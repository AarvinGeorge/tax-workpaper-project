# UX audit: redundancy, action scope and workflow friction in Taxmark

Date: Sat 17 Oct 2026, 21:18 to 21:26 EDT (01:18 to 01:26 UTC Sun 18 Oct), by `date` on this laptop.
Reviewer stance: UX lead for professional workflow tools, with US tax-return preparation and firm review in mind (preparer, reviewer, partner sign-off, tick-and-tie, review notes, client questions, e-file, amendments).
Scope: the brief (`docs/brief.txt`), the screen, shell, panel and policy code listed in the request, and a walk of the live app at `https://taxmark-workpaper.vercel.app/w/ux-audit/...` as Priya, Daniel, Helen, Jo, the IRS examiner and Ngozi. One adjustment was proposed as Priya to put real work in the workspace; the story clock was moved as Jo to 26 Jan 2028 to see the examiner; the workspace was reset at the end ("Reset to start", "Confirm reset" as Daniel) and the reset was read back: story time 23 Jun 2026, "v3, none of our changes applied yet", history "Nothing recorded in this workspace yet".
Line references are to `project-build/src/...`. Screenshots are in `.playwright-mcp/ux-01-*.jpg` to `ux-21-*.jpg` at the project root. No full SSN was recorded. Nothing in the project was edited except this file.

The owner liked one earlier fix (Checks: a separate "Select for sign-off" list became a checkbox column in the one table with a required per-row note). This audit looks for more of the same kind: one thing shown or offered in two places, actions whose target is unclear, and steps the intended workflow has to fight.

## 1. The owner's three questions

### Q1. Review versus Checks: separate, merged, or re-cut?

What each is today, from the code and the walk:

| | Checks (`work/Checks.tsx`) | Review (`work/Review.tsx`) |
|---|---|---|
| Unit | A finding (our check that fired) and the engine's diagnostics | The return as a whole: approval state, plus tabs over adjustments, review notes, sign-offs, partner items |
| Unique content | Severity filter, per-finding explanation and evidence, "Clear with a reason", AI suggestions, engine diagnostics | Approve button and its blocker reason, open review notes with Resolve, the sign-off register with stale marks, the partner-threshold list, "Review note on the return" |
| Shared content | The blocker rows (10 today) | The same 10 blockers as a "Blocking approval" list (`Review.tsx:77-87`), same hrefs |
| Sign-off | Checkbox column, per-row note, bulk bar, one undo | Per-adjustment "Review note / Sign off as reviewer" form in the Changes tab (`Review.tsx:134-137`) |

Recommendation: keep them separate, but re-cut the overlap so that each owns one thing.

- Checks is where findings are worked: read, trace, clear, sign off. It should keep bulk sign-off (it is the only multi-item place) and the engine diagnostics beside our checks.
- Review is the gate: "can this return be approved, and what is in the way". It should own the approval button, the register of what has been done (adjustments with their status, notes, sign-offs, partner items), and the Resolve action on notes. It should not be a second place to sign off items; remove the per-row sign-off form from its Changes tab and link each row to the panel instead (finding F2).
- The blocker list in Review is a summary that links to the place of action, so it is not redundancy by the owner's own test. Keep it, but make it say what clears each entry (today the list is bare titles; see finding F7).
- Rename the Review tab "Changes" to "Adjustments". Its own box label already says "Our adjustments over v3" (`Review.tsx:101`), and "Changes" is also the name of the screen that holds the full event log, so one word means two lists (finding F5).
- The one HIGH item underneath: the Review Adjustments tab today lists only applied adjustments, so a preparer's proposal never reaches the reviewer there, and the only Apply button lives in that tab (finding F1).

### Q2. The right panel's actions: does a user know which single item an action applies to?

Not reliably. The panel does hold one claim at a time (`?claim=`), and the head names it. But three things work against the reader:

1. Distance. For a line, the order is: head (pinned), engine note, "How it was built", up to three source pages with highlighted text, "Where it came from" (the same sources again as a list), "Our checks on this" (the finding in full), "From the engine", "Who checked it" (sign-offs, notes, client answers, history), then "Actions" (`ClaimDrawer.tsx:247-271`). On SchA.5b that is two full screens of scrolling before "Actions", and the heading is the bare word "Actions" (`DrawerActions.tsx:69`); the object is not restated.
2. Silent re-targeting. Almost everything between the head and the actions is a link that swaps the panel's claim without a trace: source items go to a document (`:98`), "Inputs" go to other lines (`:251`), "Our checks on this" goes to a finding (`:203`), "Sits on" goes back to a line (`:379`). A reviewer who clicks "Seller credit for 2025 taxes" to check the evidence, then scrolls to "Sign off as reviewer", signs off the document D54, not the line SchA.5b they were reviewing. Nothing says so except the small head text, now off-screen (for documents and findings the head is not pinned; only `LineClaim` uses `drw-head`).
3. One action set for three kinds of object. The same seven buttons appear on a line, a document and a finding (`ClaimDrawer.tsx:124-128`). "Adjust" is shown disabled on documents and findings (seen on D54 and on the finding panel: `button "Adjust" [disabled]`), "Record a decision" and "Ask the client" are offered on a document, and the document also has its own "Add a note" box above the actions, so there are two note actions on one object with different meanings (`DocNotes` at `:340-362`, `review_note` at `DrawerActions.tsx:98-101`). "Assign to" lists the viewer themself and Jo and Sam, who do not do return work (`DrawerActions.tsx:129-132`, seen as Daniel).

What the panel's action scope should look like (smallest change first):

- Put the action bar directly under the pinned head, for all three kinds, and name the object in it: "Actions on SchA.5b, Real estate taxes (home)". Pin the head for documents and findings as for lines.
- Per kind, show only actions that policy can ever allow for that kind; keep "disabled with a reason" for role and state rules only. Line: Adjust, Verify, Sign off, Review note, Ask the client, Ask a colleague, Record a decision. Document: Verify, Add a note, Ask the client, Ask a colleague, Review note. Finding: Clear with a reason, Sign off, Review note, Ask the client, Ask a colleague, Record a decision. Drop the dead "Adjust" on documents and findings.
- When the panel moves to another claim by an in-panel link, show a one-line "Back to SchA.5b" under the head, so the reader knows the target changed and can return.
- Remove self, Jo and Sam from "Assign to".

Do not change: one claim per panel, no stacking, the server re-check of every action, the sticky number for lines.

## 2. Findings, ordered by severity

Severity: HIGH invites a wrong action or a missed review step; MED slows or confuses; LOW is polish.

### F1. A proposed adjustment never reaches the reviewer's Review screen, and nothing can apply it. HIGH

Screens and roles: Review (reviewer, partner), panel (all staff), Areas.

What I saw. As Priya, on `return?claim=line:SchA.5b`, Adjust, 6508, reason, authority, Save: status "Adjustment proposed and saved; a reviewer applies it. The original value is kept." As Daniel, Review shows "Changes 0" and "No adjustments yet. Adjust a number from its drawer (open any amount)", while the sidebar says "Priya adjusted Real estate taxes (home)", the bell says "Priya proposed a change to Real estate taxes (home) for your review" and links to `review?claim=line:SchA.5b`. That panel's head says "As computed, v3 · 13,420"; its History row says "Priya Raman adjustment to 6,508: Remove the seller's share..." without the word "proposed", and there is no Apply. The Changes screen does show "6,508 ... (proposed)" with an Undo. Daniel's banner promises "apply proposed adjustments".

Why, in the code: `Review.tsx:40` lists `claims.filter((c) => c.adjusted)`; `project.ts:86` sets `adjusted: applied.length > 0`; `events.ts:56` `isApplied` excludes `status === 'proposed'`. The only Apply button is inside that filtered list (`Review.tsx:129-133`).

Why it hurts. The preparer-proposes, reviewer-applies rule (`policy.ts:173`) is the firm's core control, and the reviewer's screen for it is empty. The reviewer follows the notice, sees "As computed", and either misses the proposal or records a fresh "Adjust" of their own (as reviewer it saves as applied), which loses Priya's reason and authority and leaves her proposal dangling.

Reconciliation (smallest change). In `Review.tsx`, list claims that have any live adjustment event, proposed or applied (the row already has the Proposed/Applied tag and the Apply button). Apply the same to `Areas.tsx:298-300`. In the panel head, when the last live adjustment is proposed, show "Proposed 6,508 by Priya, awaiting reviewer" beside the computed value, and put Apply in the panel's trail for reviewer and partner (one more `EventButton`, same event as Review's). Make the Changes screen's "(proposed)" a tag, not a suffix.

Do not change: who may propose and who may apply; the original value staying visible.

### F2. Sign-off is offered in three places, and "Review note" means two different things. HIGH

Screens and roles: Checks, panel, Review (preparer, reviewer, partner).

What I saw. Checks row: "Review note for sign-off REQUIRED" input, then the sticky bar "Sign off selected (n)" (`actions.tsx:157-169, 126-141`). Finding panel: button "Sign off as reviewer" opens "Review note (required)" (`DrawerActions.tsx:95`). Review Changes tab: a per-row "Review note" field whose submit button is "Sign off as reviewer" (`Review.tsx:135`). Separately, "Review note" is also the name of the action that creates a `review_note` event with a "Blocking: open blocking notes stop approval" checkbox (`DrawerActions.tsx:98-101`), and Review's own "Review note on the return" with "Blocks approval" (`Review.tsx:195-200`).

Why it hurts. A reviewer who wants to send an item back types their note into the nearest "Review note" field and presses the button under it, which signs the item off. The opposite mistake also exists: a sign-off note typed as a "Review note" blocks filing. The firm rule "Open review notes block filing" makes this an approval-gating confusion, not a wording nit.

Reconciliation. One word per meaning. The sign-off field is "What you checked" (in `SignoffNote`, `DrawerActions` signoff panel and Review), and the button is "Sign off as reviewer". "Review note" is reserved for the note that can block, and its button says "Leave a review note". Remove the inline sign-off form from Review's Adjustments tab; the row links to the panel, which already signs a line. Keep Checks (bulk, findings) and the panel (single, any claim).

Do not change: the required note on every sign-off; bulk sign-off with one undo.

### F3. The document panel says "No open checks" on the document that is the evidence of a blocker. HIGH

Screens and roles: panel on Documents and from any evidence link (all staff).

What I saw. From the blocker "Real estate taxes (home) includes the other party's share credited at closing", the evidence link "Seller credit for 2025 taxes (D54, page 1)" opens D54. The panel says "Our checks on this · 0", "No open checks", "No check in the library fails on this claim." `FindingsOn` matches `f.claims.includes(claimId)` only (`ClaimDrawer.tsx:192`); evidence refs are not consulted.

Why it hurts. Tick-and-tie runs from the document side as often as from the line side. A reviewer opening D54 to see whether the closing statement is clean is told it is, while a blocker rests on it.

Reconciliation. In `FindingsOn`, also match findings whose evidence resolves (`refClaim`) to this document, under the label "Checks that cite this document · n". Keep the line-side match as is.

### F4. The client's Filing screen contradicts itself about the e-file signature, and "Needed" asks outlive the filing. HIGH

Screens and roles: Filing ("Approve and sign") and Home, client.

What I saw. As Ngozi at story time 26 Jan 2028: "What has happened: 10 Jul 2026, You approved the return and signed the e-file authorization" (from the packet timeline, `Filing.tsx:134`), then two sections down "Approve and sign: Your return has been filed, so signing is closed. Ngozi Okafor, Not signed. Erik Lindqvist, Not signed" (from workspace `client_signature` events, `ClientFiling.tsx`). Home still lists three "Needed" items (`page.tsx:116`), including "Please send a photo of the card", eighteen months after acceptance; the bell and "Since you last looked" show "we need a document from you".

Why it hurts. The client is told in one breath that they signed and that they have not. "Not signed" against a filed return reads as a problem with their return. Open asks after acceptance invite the client to send documents nobody will act on.

Reconciliation. One source of truth per fact: when the packet timeline carries the 8879 event and the workspace has no signature events, show "Signed 10 Jul 2026 (firm record)" in the signing rows, or hide the rows once frozen and keep the timeline line. On client Home, show `plain` findings only while the return is open and the finding is not adjusted or cleared (`findingStatus` exists), and drop the post-filing ask from the notice set once accepted.

Do not change: the plain-words wording; the no-PIN rule.

### F5. Four names for logs: History, Changes, Changes tab, Timeline, Since you last looked. MED

Screens and roles: every screen (staff).

What I saw. Sidebar "History" (last 8 events, every screen); nav "Changes · Every action: why, authority, effect, who"; Review tab "Changes" (applied adjustments only, 0 today while the Changes screen shows 1); panel "History · 1"; Filing "Timeline to story time" (the packet's events, not ours); Home "Since you last looked". The version string says "v3 plus n adjustments", Review's h1 says "and our adjustments", Changes' h1 says "Our changes over the packet".

Why it hurts. A new hire cannot tell which list is the audit trail, which is theirs, and which is the engine's. The Review tab count disagreeing with the Changes screen count is read as a bug.

Reconciliation. Settle the vocabulary: an "adjustment" is a value change; the "Changes" screen is every action (keep the name, the brief uses "changes"); the sidebar is "Recent" with an "All" link to Changes; the Review tab is "Adjustments"; Filing's section is "The season, from the packet". Leave the panel's "History" (it is scoped to one item).

## 3. Proposed information architecture

One sentence: the Return and Documents are the two ways in, the panel is the one place a number is read and acted on, Checks is the work queue of findings, Review is the gate and the register, Changes is the audit log, Areas and Filing are the records a CPA and the client expect, Firm is the firm's own view, and Findings is the hand-in ledger. Each screen owns one job and links out for the rest; notices live in the bell; sign-off happens where the item is (Checks for findings in bulk, the panel for any single item); approval happens on Review only.

- Quoting the firm rule where it bites (Review, panel reveal reason, Helen's banner).
