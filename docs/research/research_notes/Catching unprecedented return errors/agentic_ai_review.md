# Agentic AI reviewers for tax/audit data, and turning AI findings into deterministic rules

Research date: 24 Oct 2026 (session clock). About 15 tool calls of web research. Unless marked "full text read", findings come from search-result summaries of the cited pages, and marketing claims are labelled as such. Context: our workpaper app has deterministic checks plus a per-document AI reader (13 of 27 known mistakes). A blind agentic run with a Python tool over the whole packet found 22 of 27 plus one new error (state AMT never computed).

## Q1. Which products do agentic review today, what tools do they give the agent, and what is deterministic vs model judgement?

### Takeaway
By 2025-2026 every major tax/audit vendor and all Big Four firms ship "agents". The common pattern is an LLM that orchestrates over deterministic engines and ledgers (tax calc engines, journal-entry analytics, source extraction with citations), with a human reviewer signing off. None of the sources I found publish recall/precision for the agent's *review* function. The numbers that exist are self-reported time savings or accuracy figures with no stated method.

### Cited Findings
- **Thomson Reuters CoCounsel (tax, audit, accounting):** launched June 2025 as TR's "first agentic system", positioned as going beyond prompt-and-response chat — [Accountancy Age, Jun 2025](https://accountancyage.com/2025/06/02/thomson-reuters-launches-agentic-ai-for-tax-and-accounting-professionals/?amp=1). Nov 2025: "Ready to Review" automates 1040 preparation from source documents and prior-year returns, producing returns "ready for professional review". A firm (CLH CPAs) reports about one hour saved per simple 1040 (customer-reported). "CoCounsel Document Analysis" for audit extracts data for vouching and substantive testing and produces *cited* workpapers; it was announced for Jan 2026 and I could not confirm it shipped — [TR press release, 5 Nov 2025](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en/press-releases/2025/november/thomson-reuters-advances-ai-market-leadership-with-new-agentic-ai-solutions). Grounded in Checkpoint content and primary sources (IRS, FASB, AICPA, IFRS). Claims use at over 1,300 firms. Won a Stevie Award in Apr 2026 (a press award, not an evaluation) — [TR, 23 Apr 2026](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en-us/posts/innovation/thomson-reuters-honored-as-stevie-award-winner-for-cocounsel-tax-and-audit/).
- **Basis (accounting/tax/audit agents):** OpenAI case study, Aug 2025. A supervising agent (o3, later GPT-5) routes work to sub-agents; GPT-4.1 handles quick clarifications and GPT-5 handles heavier reasoning such as unusual transaction patterns. Claims "up to 30%" time saved (vendor-published) — [OpenAI customer story](https://openai.com/index/basis). Secondary coverage says Basis exposes the data sources, assumptions and confidence behind each recommendation for auditability — [llmbase.ai](https://llmbase.ai/news/openai-o3-gpt-5-and-gpt-4-1-power-basis-ai-agents-for-accounting-automation). Raised $100M at a $1.15B valuation in Feb 2026, pitched as "end-to-end agents across accounting, tax, and audit"; firms report 20-50% efficiency gains (company-reported) — [BusinessWire, 24 Feb 2026](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260224020999/en/Basis-Raises-$100M-at-a-$1.15B-Valuation-as-Accounting-Firms-Adopt-End-to-End-Agents-Across-Accounting,-Tax,-and-Audit).
- **Black Ore Tax Autopilot (1040 prep):** claims accuracy above 99% across "tens of thousands" of pilot returns and up to 98% time saved. No method published (self-reported). Output goes through a preliminary review by Black Ore's US-based CPAs before going back to the firm, so a human stays in the loop — [Sovereign Magazine](https://www.sovereignmagazine.com/article/black-ore-tax-autopilot-launch); [CPA Practice Advisor, 2023 launch](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2023/11/07/black-ore-launches-with-60-million-in-funding-to-bring-ai-to-financial-services/97324/). - **MindBridge (audit analytics, pre-dates LLM agents):** an explicit "ensemble" of hard-coded expert business rules, statistical tests (z-score, 2-digit Benford, rare vendor-account) and unsupervised ML, scoring *every* journal entry. Positioned for AS 2401 / AU-C 240 / ISA 240 journal-entry testing. No independent accuracy data found; all sources are vendor material — [MindBridge platform](https://www.mindbridge.ai/platform/); [JE Testing memo (PDF)](https://www.mindbridge.ai/docs/library/MindBridge_for_Journal_Entry_Testing_Memo.pdf).
- **Big Four:** KPMG built agents into Clara (Apr 2025) for substantive procedures such as expense vouching and the search for unrecorded liabilities, then trialled "orchestration agents" that oversee other AI tools — [Accounting Today](https://www.accountingtoday.com/news/kpmg-builds-ai-agents-into-audit-platform); [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/esg/kpmg-to-test-advanced-ai-agents-in-next-phase-for-audits). Deloitte (Zora, 2025) rolled out autonomous audit agents for data crunching in late June 2026 — [Bloomberg Tax](https://news.bgov.com/daily-tax-report/deloitte-adopts-advanced-ai-to-power-corporate-audits-globally). ### Inferences
- The deterministic/judgement split across products is consistent. Deterministic: calculation (tax engines), full-population ledger tests (rules, statistics), and extraction tied to a cited source. Model judgement: routing, picking what to look at, explaining anomalies, and drafting review notes. This matches our design (engine + checks deterministic; AI as reader/reviewer).
- The tools these agents get are retrieval over source documents and prior-year returns, ledger/journal-entry query, a calculation engine, and authoritative research content. Our blind run's toolset (lines, engine rules, agency records, Python) is the same shape.
- No vendor publishes recall on a seeded-error set. Our 22/27 vs 13/27 comparison is a kind of evidence the market itself lacks, so it is worth presenting with its method (one packet, one run).

### Gaps
- No published recall/precision for any commercial tax- or audit-review agent. Black Ore's ">99%" has no stated method.
- The exact tool schemas exposed to the CoCounsel, Basis and Big Four agents are not public.
- I could not confirm that CoCounsel Document Analysis (audit) shipped in Jan 2026.
- Intuit (Intuit Assist / agents), Rillet, Numeric and Trullion were not researched for lack of budget.

## Q2. Published evals of LLM agents on tax/accounting tasks: accuracy, variance, failure modes

### Takeaway
On tax *calculation*, frontier models alone are unreliable and inconsistent run to run. The best result in TaxCalcBench (2025) is about 32% of returns exactly right; scores fall further when every repeated run must be right. The authors conclude deterministic engines remain necessary. Finance-agent benchmarks with tools score only about 52-65% in 2026. Both results support "LLM finds, engine computes."

### Cited Findings
- **TaxCalcBench (Column Tax, Jul 2025; full text read):** 51 federal-only TY2024 returns with hand-built inputs, and expected outputs verified by a deterministic tax engine. Strict (exact) / lenient (±$5 per line) correct-return rates: Gemini 2.5 Pro 32.35% / 51.96%; Claude Opus 4 27.45% / 42.65%; Gemini 2.5 Flash 25.98% / 41.18%; Claude Sonnet 4 23.04% / 38.24%. Each model ran 4 times at each of 5 thinking levels, and pass^k falls as k grows (inconsistent across runs). Failure modes: bracket math used instead of the IRS tax tables (about 15-20% of cases), arithmetic errors, wrong eligibility (CTC, EITC), and cascading errors such as hallucinated line numbers. The setup is text-only with no tools stated. Conclusion: findings "point to a continued need for deterministic tax calculation engines" plus orchestration infrastructure around LLMs — [arXiv 2507.16126](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.16126v1). A secondary blog cites GPT-5 at 41.7% (unverified against the paper) — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/rawveg/when-ai-files-your-taxes-5cfp).
- **Vals AI Finance Agent (tool-using agent, SEC-filing analyst tasks):** v2 top scores reported between about 52% (GPT-5.5, May 2026) and 60.6% (Aug 2026); a Jul 2026 BenchLM snapshot lists Claude Opus 5 at 58.6%. On v1.1, Claude Opus 4.7 leads at 64.37%. The sources are secondary aggregators that conflict by date, and v1.1 and v2 scores are not comparable — [BenchLM v2](https://www.benchlm.ai/benchmarks/financeAgentV2); [BenchLM v1.1](https://benchlm.ai/benchmarks/valsfinanceagentv11); [allmind, Aug 2026](https://allmind.ai/research/ai-financial-research-benchmarks-explained); [KuCoin blog](https://kucoin.com/blog/can-ai-replace-financial-analysts-in-2026-vals-ai-finance-agent-v2-reveals-gpt-5-5-hits-just-52-percent-accuracy).
- **TaxPraBen (2026):** a benchmark for LLMs in Chinese real-world tax practice — [arXiv 2604.08948](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.08948) (not read; listed for completeness).

### Inferences
- Our blind agentic run did not ask the model to *compute* the return. It recomputed with a Python tool against the engine's rules and compared. That sidesteps the arithmetic and tax-table failure modes TaxCalcBench documents, which is likely why its recall was high.
- Run-to-run variance (pass^k falling) means a single agentic run's 22/27 is one sample. Before claiming a recall figure, run it several times and report the union, the intersection and the per-run spread.
- No public benchmark measures *error-finding* (seeded-mistake recall) on a tax return. TaxCalcBench measures calculation. A seeded-mistake packet like ours is a different, unpublished eval type.

### Gaps
- No published variance figures for tool-using agents on tax review. The TaxCalcBench pass^k values appear only in a figure I could not read.
- FinanceBench (Patronus, 2023, older) was not re-checked for 2026 results.

## Q3. Patterns and evidence for LLMs generating rules/tests that become deterministic, and how they are validated before adoption

### Takeaway
The strongest pattern in practice is "LLM proposes, deterministic filters dispose, human accepts" (Meta's TestGen-LLM). Accumulating verified code skills (Voyager) or workflows (AWM) measurably improves agents. The main documented failure mode is that generated checks encode the *observed* behaviour rather than the *correct* behaviour (the oracle problem), and many assertions are trivial. A generated rule therefore has to be proven to fire on the bad case and stay silent on good ones (mutation-style) before it is promoted.

### Cited Findings
- **Meta TestGen-LLM (FSE 2024; older but the canonical industrial case):** "Assured Offline LLM-based Software Engineering". A candidate test is discarded unless it builds, passes repeatedly (a flakiness filter) and adds coverage. Survivors go to engineers as diffs. It improved 11.5% of the classes it was applied to, and engineers accepted 73% of its recommendations for production — [arXiv 2402.09171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09171v1); filter stages per [ZenML summary](https://www.zenml.io/llmops-database/automated-unit-test-improvement-using-llms-for-android-applications).
- **Voyager (NeurIPS 2023; older):** a skill library of *executable code*, where a program is added only after environment feedback and self-verification show it succeeds. Skills are stored in a vector DB and retrieved by description, and are described as "interpretable and compositional", reducing catastrophic forgetting. 3.3x more unique items and tech-tree milestones up to 15.3x faster than prior SOTA; the library transfers to a new world — [arXiv 2305.16291](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.16291).
- **Agent Workflow Memory (ICML 2025):** induces reusable workflows from past trajectories (offline from training examples, or online from the agent's own outputs that an evaluator judges correct) and feeds back the relevant ones. +24.6% and +51.1% relative success on Mind2Web and WebArena, with fewer steps — [PMLR v267](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/wang25bx.html); [arXiv 2409.07429](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.07429v1).
- **LLM-written data-validation tests:** GPT-3.5/GPT-4 benchmarked against a gold-standard validation suite, with "considerable returns" from few-shot examples — [arXiv 2310.01402](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01402v2). A fine-tuned LLaMA-3.2-3B (LoRA) turns natural-language data-quality requirements into Great Expectations rules, beating generic models (SEAA 2025) — [Quality by Prompt](https://staff.najah.edu/en/publications/18414/). A template-guided approach with an LLM judge and a domain-judge filter recovered 75-100% of expert "golden" rules plus extra constraints (TU Wien thesis, 2025) — [repositum](https://repositum.tuwien.at/handle/20.500.12708/221852). Prompt chaining beat model choice for rule quality (Jyväskylä thesis, 2025) — [JYX](https://jyx.jyu.fi/jyx/Record/jyx_123456789_102965/Description). A three-stage framework of statistical filtering, then LLM rule and code generation with RAG — [arXiv 2509.10572](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2509.10572).
- **Failure mode — oracles mirror actual, not expected, behaviour:** LLMs are more likely to write assertions that capture what the code *does* than what it *should* do — [arXiv 2410.21136](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.21136v1). On 13,866 oracles from 135 Java projects created after the models' training cutoffs, LLM oracles reached a 43% average mutation score vs 45% for human-written ones, and struggled with complex oracles — [Molinelli et al., ASE 2025](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~mernst/pubs/neurosymbolic-oracles-ase2025-abstract.html). In MUTGEN, 74% of unfixed tests failed because of wrong or incomplete oracles — [arXiv 2506.02954](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.02954). Recurring trivial or tautological assertions (non-null checks, mirroring the implementation) are reported in the GEM framework paper, per a search snippet I could not confirm.
- **Production precedent for a mixed rule set:** MindBridge runs expert-authored rules alongside statistical and ML layers on the full population. It is an existence proof that hard rules and learned detection coexist in audit, though its rules are human-written, not LLM-induced — [MindBridge](https://www.mindbridge.ai/platform/).

### Inferences
- A safe promotion pipeline for the workpaper, adapted from TestGen-LLM, Voyager and the oracle literature:
  1. The agent proposes a candidate check as code plus a plain-language rationale and the packet fields it reads.
  2. Deterministic gates: it is pure (no model call at run time); it **fires** on the packet where the error was found; it **stays silent** on a corrected copy of that packet and on other clean returns (a fire-and-silence or mutation test); and it is stable across repeated runs.
  3. A human reviewer (preparer/reviewer role) accepts it, with a citation to the governing rule (IRC, form instructions).
  4. It lands as a regression test in `project-build/tests/`, like the existing one-test-per-mistake rule.
- Overfitting risk: a rule induced from one household (e.g. "CA AMT line missing when ISO exercised") can be hard-coded to this packet's values. Require it to be stated over field types and tax rules, not literal amounts, and test it against at least one synthetic variant.
- The oracle problem maps directly. If the agent writes the rule by observing the engine's output, it can encode the engine's bug as "expected". The state-AMT finding is exactly such a case, where the engine never computed a line. Rules must cite the external authority, not the engine.
- Rule drift: tax law changes every year, so each promoted rule needs a tax-year scope and an owner, and should be re-validated each season. This is my inference; I found no source specific to tax-rule drift.
- Keeping the agentic layer running *parallel* to deterministic checks, rather than replacing them, matches every product pattern found. The agent's value is finding things no rule covers yet. Promoted rules make the known cases cheap, reproducible and auditable.

### Gaps
- No published case of an LLM-induced rule being promoted into a *tax* or *audit* production rule set with a measured false-positive rate.
- No empirical study found on long-term drift or decay of LLM-generated validation rules.
- "Fire-and-silence" as named here is my framing of mutation testing; I found no tax-specific literature using it.

## Q4. Governance: regulators and standard setters on AI in audit/tax review, explainability and reproducibility

### Takeaway
Regulators have not banned or specifically certified AI. They apply existing duties: the practitioner stays responsible, must review AI output before it reaches the IRS or a client, and (for PCAOB audits from FY2026) must evaluate the reliability of information processed by technology tools and investigate the items those tools flag. That favours deterministic, citable, re-runnable checks as the system of record, with AI findings as leads a human verifies.

### Cited Findings
- **IRS OPR Alert 2026-19 (24 Jun 2026):** applies existing Circular 230 duties to AI-assisted work. Practitioners remain responsible for the accuracy of work submitted to the IRS or given to clients, must protect confidentiality, and must charge reasonable fees. Under §10.22 (due diligence), practitioners should "thoroughly review all AI-created material" before using it in anything presented to the IRS or a client. The alert names fabricated outputs, bias and lack of transparency as risks. It does not settle whether clients must be told about AI use — [Journal of Accountancy, Jun 2026](https://www.journalofaccountancy.com/news/2026/jun/irs-outlines-ai-risks-circular-230-duties-for-tax-practitioners/); [Let's Data Science](https://letsdatascience.com/news/irs-ai-guidance-leaves-client-disclosure-question-open-f5400ffe).
- **AICPA:** the SSTS (revised, effective 1 Jan 2024) include a due-diligence standard (SSTS 1.1.6). The Due Diligence white paper is non-authoritative. The AICPA reportedly advises disclosing AI use to clients as a precaution — [The Tax Adviser, Jun 2026](https://www.thetaxadviser.com/news/2026/jun/irs-outlines-ai-risks-circular-230-duties-for-tax-practitioners/); background in [The Tax Adviser, Feb 2024 (older)](https://www.thetaxadviser.com/issues/2024/feb/tax-ethics-and-use-of-generative-ai-systems/).
- **PCAOB technology-assisted analysis amendments (AS 1105, AS 2301):** adopted 2024 (Release 2024-007; SEC-approved 20 Aug 2024), effective for fiscal years beginning on or after 15 Dec 2025. Auditors must evaluate the relevance and reliability of information obtained or processed with technology tools. When tools flag items in tests of details, the auditor must investigate whether those items indicate misstatements or control deficiencies — [PCAOB implementation page](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/implementation-resources-PCAOB-standards-rules/amendments-related-to-aspects-of-designing-and-performing-audit-procedures-that-involve-technology-assisted-analysis-of-information-in-electronic-form); [CalCPA](https://www.calcpa.org/whats-happening/articles/ai-standard-setting-regulation-in-audit-part-1). The CAQ asked for a one-year deferral of AS 1105 ¶.10A (Jul 2025), and the PCAOB issued Policy Statement 2025-004 on implementing it; the final status is unverified — [CAQ letter](https://www.thecaq.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/CAQ-_Letter-to-PCAOB_SIGNED_TAA_RFD_7.23.25.pdf).
- **PCAOB staff GenAI Spotlight (Jul 2024; older):** firms used GenAI mainly for administrative and research work, saw potential in planning and performing audits, and stressed strong supervision. These are staff views, not Board policy, and the Spotlight gives no metrics — [PCAOB news release](https://pcaobus.org/news-events/news-releases/news-release-detail/pcaob-staff-shares-observations-from-outreach-on-use-of-generative-artificial-intelligence-in-audits-and-financial-reporting); [Spotlight PDF](https://pcaobus.org/documents/generative-ai-spotlight.pdf).
- **Professional commentary:** governance and evidencing, not capability, is now the hard problem for audit agents — [ACCA AB Magazine, Aug 2026](https://abmagazine.accaglobal.com/global/articles/2026/aug/practice/audit-s-not-so-secret-agents.html); judgement stays with the professional — [NYSSCPA, Jun 2026](https://nysscpa.org/news/1052350-professional-judgement-in-the-age-of-ai-2026-06-01).

### Inferences
- "Must investigate flagged items" (AS 2301) and "review all AI-created material" (§10.22) both imply that an agent's findings need a review state (open, confirmed, dismissed with reason) and a stored trail. A run that cannot be re-performed is weak evidence, so the deterministic rules distilled from agent findings are what make a finding reproducible.
- Explainability is easiest when each finding carries source document, field, the recomputation and the governing authority, as our workpaper already does. An agent should be required to emit findings in that same schema.

### Gaps
- I found no 2025-2026 PCAOB statement specific to *agentic* or generative AI in audit beyond the 2024 Spotlight; one may exist.
- I did not read the full text of IRS OPR Alert 2026-19 (only Journal of Accountancy and Tax Adviser coverage).
- No regulator guidance found on AI-*generated* rules or tests becoming part of a firm's methodology (validation, change control).
