# Audit analytics and financial-close patterns for surfacing unanticipated errors deterministically

Research date: Sat 24 Oct 2026. Sources fetched or searched in this pass. Vendor claims are labelled VENDOR; regulator or standard text is labelled STANDARD; independent academic or practitioner material is labelled INDEPENDENT. Material dated before 2024 is marked [older].

Context carried from the brief: the app missed a state AMT line that the engine *said* it never computed, left at 0 while its input was 40,000. The inferences below map each pattern to that miss.

## 1. How the commercial tools work: deterministic control points vs statistical/ML, and how flags are prioritised and explained

### Takeaway
The leading tools share one architecture. They run a battery of named tests over 100% of the population. Most tests are deterministic rules or simple statistics, with some ML. Each item gets a weighted score, and the system shows *which named test fired* for each flagged item. Close tools (BlackLine, FloQast) are almost entirely deterministic: every account must reconcile within a threshold or it cannot be certified. The pattern that catches the unforeseen is "everything must be accounted for, and any gap is an open item." It is not "detect known bad patterns."

### Cited Findings
- **MindBridge, architecture (VENDOR, 2023 whitepaper [older but current product line]):** "the platform will analyze all transactions and then automatically assign risk scores to each," using "Ensemble AI," which "simultaneously compares your data against different combinations of rules-based tests, statistical methods, and machine learning techniques." — [MindBridge AI Audit Methodology Whitepaper (2023)](https://info.mindbridge.ai/hubfs/docs-website/Whitepaper/MindBridgeAnalytics-AI-Audit-Methodology-Whitepaper.pdf)
- **MindBridge, named control points (VENDOR):** examples include "Outlier Anomaly" (an unusual combination of date, amount, nominal account and monetary flow), "Rare Flow" (unusual ways money flows between accounts), "2 Digit Benford", "Manual Entry", "Unusual Amount" (unusual value for a particular account) and "Expert Score" (characteristics domain experts identified as risky). — [MindBridge whitepaper](https://info.mindbridge.ai/hubfs/docs-website/Whitepaper/MindBridgeAnalytics-AI-Audit-Methodology-Whitepaper.pdf)
- **MindBridge, explainability (VENDOR):** "MindBridge clearly communicates which control points have triggered for each transaction, making it easy to understand why a transaction has been flagged as high risk." Users can add or remove control points per assertion and adjust weights, and weight changes are exported to workpapers. — [MindBridge whitepaper](https://info.mindbridge.ai/hubfs/docs-website/Whitepaper/MindBridgeAnalytics-AI-Audit-Methodology-Whitepaper.pdf)
- **MindBridge, scoring formula (VENDOR support docs):** for AP/AR, the risk score is "a weighted average of all control point results for the entry." Example default weights are 2-digit Benford = 5 and ML "activity flurry" = 10. Counts differ by source: "more than 40 capabilities" on one page versus 28 control points in a support template. — [MindBridge support article](https://support.mindbridge.ai/hc/en-us/articles/360058394213); [MindBridge support template](https://support.mindbridge.ai/hc/de-de/article_attachments/4434341902871); [MindBridge platform page](https://www.mindbridge.ai/platform/)
- **MindBridge, the analytical-procedure side (VENDOR):** supports period-over-period and year-over-year changes per line item, letting auditors "develop, challenge, or confirm expectations of current year results based on prior year trends." It also supports investigating relationships "that differ from expected values by a significant amount," plus ratio pairs such as sales vs AR and interest expense vs long-term debt. — [MindBridge whitepaper](https://info.mindbridge.ai/hubfs/docs-website/Whitepaper/MindBridgeAnalytics-AI-Audit-Methodology-Whitepaper.pdf)
- **Caseware IDEA (VENDOR):** the journal-entry testing plug-in offers "over 20 journal entry audit tests." Built-ins include Benford's Law, Fuzzy Duplicate and Gap Detection, and Fuzzy Join compares two databases for duplicates. — [Caseware](https://caseware.com/uk/?p=676); [Caseware](https://www.caseware.com/?p=768)
- **Caseware Audit, completeness tie-out (VENDOR docs):** step "AI-20 Data completeness" is to "verify that the groupings totals from the trial balance agree with the groupings totals taken from imported transactions." This is a deterministic roll-forward/tie-out of detail to summary. — [Caseware docs, Risk response](https://docs.caseware.com/2020/WebApps/31/nl/Explore/Audit/Risk-response.htm)
- **DataSnipper (VENDOR):** an Excel add-in. Document Matching "finds the correct text, date and/or number in a source document, creating a reference to your sample data." Snip extracts a value and "automatically creates a reference between the two." Reviewers trace any snip to its source in one click. It is deterministic linkage of a workpaper cell to its evidence, with no anomaly scoring. — [DataSnipper, busy-season document matching](https://datasnipper.com/resources/busy-season-document-matching); [DataSnipper confirmations guide](https://support.datasnipper.com/en/articles/694175-how-to-perform-confirmation-procedures-using-datasnipper)
- **Validis (VENDOR listing):** extracts transaction-level GL/AR/AP/TB into "a single consistent schema" and runs built-in analytics including "ledger integrity" and "fraud indicators." I found no technical detail on the specific integrity checks. — [Validis app listing](https://chatgpt.com/plugins/plugin_asdk_app_6a291ec4f5448191b427235947f0dd3d)
- **FloQast, threshold tie-out (VENDOR help centre):** "Differences below the threshold set will be considered immaterial, and therefore the account ties out." A default threshold applies to new reconciliations, with per-account overrides. The data sheet says it will "Automate sign-offs on low-risk reconciliations that tie out within business-defined thresholds." — [FloQast help](https://help.floqast.com/hc/en-us/articles/360002045372); [FloQast data sheet (Mar 2024)](https://get.floqast.com/hubfs/FloQast_Data%20Sheet_Reconciliation%20Automation_March%202024.pdf)
- **BlackLine, auto-certification (VENDOR blog):** rules can be set globally or per account. Subledger and amortised support is checked against an amount threshold, a percentage difference, or both. Rules can auto-certify months 1–2 of a quarter and force manual certification in month 3. — [BlackLine blog](https://www.blackline.com/blog/improve-automate-your-account-reconciliation-process/)
- **BlackLine auto-certification rate claim:** "95%+ auto-certification on routine reconciliations" is vendor-sourced and repeated by a comparison site. Unverified. — [CFO Shortlist](https://www.cfoshortlist.com/reports/blackline-vs-floqast)

### Inferences
- The tools rarely catch the unforeseen by having smarter detectors. They catch it through a **closed-world rule**: every account or line in scope must carry a reconciliation, a support link or a score. Anything without one is an open item by construction. BlackLine and FloQast cannot certify an unreconciled account. Caseware's AI-20 makes detail tie to summary. DataSnipper makes every cell carry a reference.
- For the state AMT miss, the transferable control is **"no line may be certified while its status is not-computed or its support is absent,"** a gate on status rather than a pattern detector. This appears to be what commit `444d27a` (CHK-LINE-NOT-COMPUTED) adds. A broader generalisation is "every input with a non-zero value must flow to at least one computed output, or be explicitly marked not-applicable." That is a completeness tie-out from inputs to outputs, the analogue of Caseware AI-20.
- MindBridge's "Unusual Amount" and "Rare Flow" map onto a return as "a value unusual for this line given its inputs." For example, an output of 0 when its driving input is 40,000 is a rare flow: an input with no downstream effect.
- Explainability in every tool means naming the test that fired. It does not mean a free-text rationale. That fits a check catalogue in which each flag carries a check ID.

### Gaps
- MindBridge's exact ensemble combination formula and ML training details are not public in what I found. The control point count (28 vs 40+) varies by source and version.
- Inflo: no usable primary documentation found in this pass.
- Validis: no technical list of its "ledger integrity" checks found.
- BlackLine's handling of unreconciled differences beyond threshold logic (for example, ageing of open items) is not detailed in the sources found.

## 2. What the standards say about expectations, precision thresholds and investigating differences

### Takeaway
ISA 520 (and its US equivalent AU-C 520), the interim PCAOB AS 2305 and the 2024 PCAOB proposal all prescribe one loop. First, form an expectation independently of the recorded amount and make it precise enough, using disaggregation. Second, fix the acceptable difference (threshold) in advance; the threshold shrinks as risk rises. Third, investigate every difference above the threshold. Management explanations must be corroborated, and the full difference explained. The 2024 PCAOB amendments to AS 1105/AS 2301 extend this to technology-assisted analysis: every item the technology identifies must be investigated.

### Cited Findings
- **ISA 520 para 5(c) (STANDARD, Oct 2009 [older, still in force]):** the auditor must "develop an expectation of recorded amounts or ratios and evaluate whether the expectation is sufficiently precise" to identify a misstatement that, individually or aggregated, may cause material misstatement. — [ISA (UK and Ireland) 520, FRC](https://www.frc.org.uk/docs/2453/html); full clause at [ISA (NZ) 520, XRB](https://standards.xrb.govt.nz/standards-navigator/isa-nz-520/)
- **ISA 520 para 5(d) (STANDARD):** the auditor must "determine the amount of any difference of recorded amounts from expected values that is acceptable without further investigation." — [ISA (NZ) 520](https://standards.xrb.govt.nz/standards-navigator/isa-nz-520/)
- **ISA 520 A15 (STANDARD):** precision depends on "the degree to which information can be disaggregated" and "the accuracy with which the expected results ... can be predicted." Substantive analytical procedures "may be more effective when applied to financial information on individual sections." — [FRC ISA 520](https://www.frc.org.uk/docs/2453/html)
- **ISA 520 A16 (STANDARD):** "as the assessed risk increases, the amount of difference considered acceptable without investigation decreases." — [FRC ISA 520](https://www.frc.org.uk/docs/2453/html)
- **ISA 520 para 7 and A21 (STANDARD):** when procedures identify inconsistent fluctuations or relationships, or differences from expected values by a significant amount, the auditor must investigate by "inquiring of management and obtaining appropriate audit evidence relevant to management's responses," and perform other procedures as needed. This applies "for example, [when] management is unable to provide an explanation." — [FRC ISA 520](https://www.frc.org.uk/docs/2453/html)
- **Practitioner guidance on ISA 520 (INDEPENDENT/professional body):** the threshold is set during planning, before differences are computed, to avoid bias. "Explanations should be sought for the full amount of the difference, not just the part that exceeds the threshold." Thresholds may be numeric or percentages. — [chinaacc / ACCA F8 article](https://m.chinaacc.com/acca/fxzd/zh1511172682.shtml)
- **PCAOB AS 2305 status (STANDARD):** the current AS 2305 is an AICPA interim standard from 1989, adopted by the PCAOB on an interim basis in 2003. On 12 Jun 2024 the Board proposed a replacement, "AS 2305, Designing and Performing Substantive Analytical Procedures." Comments closed 12 Aug 2024. — [Deloitte DART (Jun 2024)](https://dart.deloitte.com/USDART/home/news/all-news/2024/jun/pcaob-proposes-replacement-standard-analytical-procedures); [PCAOB news release](https://pcaobus.org/news-events/news-releases/news-release-detail/pcaob-continues-its-modernization-drive-with-proposal-to-replace-outdated-standard-on-substantive-analytical-procedures)
- **What the PCAOB found wrong in practice (STANDARD/commentary):** auditors often lacked a precise enough expectation, and some failed to set an amount above which differences require investigation. The proposal requires the auditor to form its own expectation, "and not rely on the company's amount." It also tightens the "plausible and predictable" relationship test. — [CPA Practice Advisor (Jun 2024)](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2024/06/13/pcaob-looks-to-clarify-rules-on-substantive-analytical-procedures/106774/); [King & Spalding](https://kslaw.com/blog-posts/pcaob-issues-a-proposal-to-replace-current-standard-on-substantive-analytical-procedures)
- **Bright-line threshold controversy:** the proposal would require an investigation threshold at or below tolerable misstatement. EY argued this "significantly reduces or eliminates" acceptable variation in SAP precision, and the CAQ asked for a clearer definition of "company's amount." — [EY comment letter (Aug 2024)](https://ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-us/technical/accountinglink/documents/ey-cl24235-241us-08-12-2024.pdf); [CAQ comment letter (Aug 2024)](https://www.thecaq.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/caq_comment-letter-to-pcaob_substantive-analytical-procedures_2024-08.pdf)
- **PCAOB technology-assisted analysis amendments (STANDARD, adopted Jun 2024):** the Board amended AS 1105 and AS 2301 to clarify responsibilities when using technology-assisted analysis (TAA). The 2023 proposal had said that, for items TAA identifies, the auditor must consider "whether the identified items represent a misstatement or a control deficiency" and whether they "indicate a need for the auditor to modify its risk assessment or planned procedures." — [The Accountant (Jun 2023)](https://www.theaccountant-online.com/news/pcaob-proposes-greater-clarity-for-technology-assisted-analysis/); [PCAOB release](https://pcaobus.org/news-events/news-releases/news-release-detail/pcaob-issues-proposal-to-bring-greater-clarity-to-certain-auditor-responsibilities-when-using-technology-assisted-analysis)
- **AS 2301.49 / .50 as described by a vendor (VENDOR, 6 Jul 2026):** AS 2301.49 requires investigating items identified for further investigation, and AS 2301.50 covers the remaining population. MindBridge argues that a continuous score on every item breaks the flagged/unflagged binary, and that the threshold "is created entirely by the auditor's judgment about where to set the score cutoff." It asks for guidance that thresholds be set before reviewing output, tied to risk and assertions, and documented. It cites the adopting release: "it was not possible to specify scenarios that would provide sufficient appropriate audit evidence" from a clean full-population result. — [MindBridge blog (Jul 2026)](https://www.mindbridge.ai/blog/pcaob-ai-audit-strategic-priorities/)
- **SAS 142, Audit Evidence (STANDARD):** effective for periods ending on or after 15 Dec 2022 and supersedes AU-C 500. It recognises "automated tools and techniques such as audit data analytics, AI and remote observation tools." Evidence attributes (relevance, reliability, whether information corroborates or contradicts assertions) apply to those tools. Contradictory information may be relevant even from a less reliable source. — [Journal of Accountancy (Jul 2020)](https://www.journalofaccountancy.com/news/2020/jul/audit-evidence-standard-enables-technology-aided-audit-procedures.html); [Controllers Council](https://controllerscouncil.org/evolving-audit-evidence-standard-sas-no-142/); [Accounting Today](https://accountingtoday.com/news/aicpa-modernizes-audit-evidence-standard-for-technology-use)

### Inferences
- "Develop an independent expectation, compare, investigate above a pre-set threshold" maps directly onto a tax return. For each computed line, compute an **expectation by an independent route** (for example, the state AMT tentative minimum tax approximated from the AMT adjustment input times the state rate, or the prior-year relationship). Compare it with the engine's value and investigate any gap above a threshold fixed in advance. Expected ≈ 40,000 × rate against recorded 0 would have breached any threshold. This catches the AMT miss without anyone having anticipated "the engine skips a line."
- The PCAOB's specific warning "do not rely on the company's amount" applies here: the expectation must not come from the same engine whose output is being tested.
- A16 (lower threshold as risk rises) supports a zero-tolerance threshold for lines the engine itself marks uncertain or not-computed.
- The "explain the full difference" rule argues against partial resolution: a reviewer note that explains part of a variance should not clear it.

### Gaps
- Whether the PCAOB adopted the replacement AS 2305 in 2025–2026: no evidence of adoption found. The PCAOB project pages returned 403, and every result still showed the 2024 proposal as the latest action. Treat it as unresolved.
- The AS 1105/AS 2301 TAA amendments' effective date was not confirmed in a fetched source. From memory it is fiscal years beginning on or after 15 Dec 2025; verify before citing.
- AICPA *Guide to Audit Data Analytics* (2017 [older]): no primary text retrieved, so its content on expectations and thresholds is not cited here.
- Verbatim AS 2305 interim text (paras .11–.21): the PCAOB site returned 403.

## 3. Assertion-based coverage: how tools track that nothing is left unsupported

### Takeaway
Coverage is tracked as a matrix of line or account × assertion (completeness, existence/occurrence, accuracy, valuation, and so on). Every cell must be addressed by a procedure, a reconciliation or a control point, and gaps are visible as unaddressed cells. In close tools the matrix is simpler: each account carries a reconciliation status and certification, and uncertified accounts block close. Completeness is the assertion most often tested by tie-out (detail to summary) rather than by inspection.

### Cited Findings
- **MindBridge, assertion mapping (VENDOR):** the whitepaper's section 2 is "Exploring assertion-based risk scoring." Control points can be added to or removed from "specific assertions and custom risk scores." Random or risk-stratified samples are created "when testing for occurrence, classification, accuracy, or valuation assertions." — [MindBridge whitepaper](https://info.mindbridge.ai/hubfs/docs-website/Whitepaper/MindBridgeAnalytics-AI-Audit-Methodology-Whitepaper.pdf)
- **Caseware, completeness by tie-out (VENDOR):** AI-20 checks that trial balance grouping totals agree with totals from imported transactions. — [Caseware docs](https://docs.caseware.com/2020/WebApps/31/nl/Explore/Audit/Risk-response.htm)
- **Caseware IDEA, completeness and existence tests (VENDOR):** Gap Detection (missing sequence numbers, a completeness test) and Fuzzy Duplicate (double-counting, an existence/occurrence test) are standard built-ins. — [Caseware](https://caseware.com/uk/?p=676)
- **BlackLine/FloQast, per-account certification (VENDOR):** every account has a reconciliation that either ties within threshold (auto-certified) or requires preparer and reviewer sign-off. Certification cadence is configurable per account. — [BlackLine blog](https://www.blackline.com/blog/improve-automate-your-account-reconciliation-process/); [FloQast help](https://help.floqast.com/hc/en-us/articles/360002045372)
- **DataSnipper, evidence linkage (VENDOR):** every snipped cell carries a reference to its source document, so "reviewers can trace a snip back to its source document with a click." An unreferenced cell is visibly unsupported. — [DataSnipper](https://datasnipper.com/resources/busy-season-document-matching)
- **ISA 520 A15 on disaggregation (STANDARD):** precision improves when procedures apply to "individual sections" rather than the whole. That is the standards basis for per-line rather than total-level coverage. — [FRC ISA 520](https://www.frc.org.uk/docs/2453/html)

### Inferences
- For the workpaper, the coverage matrix is each return line × {sourced (existence), complete (every relevant input flowed in), computed (accuracy), reviewed}. A line whose "computed" cell is empty, such as the state AMT line the engine marked not-computed, is an unsupported cell. A coverage view would show it with no special check written for it.
- The input-side completeness test is the return's analogue of Gap Detection and the AI-20 tie-out. Every extracted value with a non-zero amount must be consumed by some computed line, or explicitly marked not-applicable with a reason. The 40,000 AMT input would show as consumed by a line that produced 0 under a not-computed status.
- Certification gating (BlackLine/FloQast) suggests approval should be structurally impossible while any line × assertion cell is open. This beats a long list of individual checks that each must be anticipated.

### Gaps
- None of the vendor sources fetched show their actual assertion-coverage UI or data model in detail.
- I found no primary source describing how audit methodologies (Big Four or AICPA) formally encode the assertion × account matrix in software.

## 4. Evidence of effectiveness, and known failure modes (false-positive floods)

### Takeaway
Independent evidence for effectiveness is thin and mostly experimental. The best-documented risk is behavioural. High false-positive rates erode auditor skepticism, and full-population testing on client-internal data can make auditors *less* skeptical of external red flags. Regulators also note that a "clean" full-population score does not by itself establish sufficient evidence. Vendor effectiveness claims (such as auto-certification rates) are unverified.

### Cited Findings
- **False positives reduce skepticism (INDEPENDENT, experimental, Barr-Pulliam, Brazel, McCallen and Walker, c. 2020 [older]):** in an experiment with practising auditors, when false-positive rates in analytics output were higher, skepticism levels were low. The study also tested whether consistent rewards for skepticism counteract this. I could not open the venue page (Cloudflare block), so treat the journal and publication status as unconfirmed. Brazel presented related work at UvA in April 2025. — [ScholarSpace record](https://scholarspace.manoa.hawaii.edu/handle/10125/70450); [UvA seminar listing (Apr 2025)](https://abs.uva.nl/content/events/accounting/04-2025/accounting-research-seminar-with-joseph-brazel-caroline-state-university.html)
- **Full-population testing can reduce skepticism (INDEPENDENT, experimental, Anna Gold, VU Amsterdam, et al.):** full-population testing raises the quantity of evidence without necessarily improving quality, and relies on client-internal data vulnerable to manipulation. Auditors using it were *less* likely than sample-testers to act skeptically on an external industry-data fraud red flag. A predicted worsening from visualisation was not supported. This is a seminar abstract; publication venue unconfirmed. — [IESEG conference listing](https://www.ieseg.fr/en/events/conference-an-unintended-consequence-of-full-population-testing-on-auditors-professional-skepticism/)
- **Base-rate problem (INDEPENDENT, general):** when true positives are rare, "the vast majority of all positive cases will be false positives" in screening at scale. — [EFF slide](https://w2.eff.org/Privacy/Surveillance/KDL-datamining/slide40.html); [arXiv base-rate review (2022)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.08801)
- **Regulatory caution on what a clean full-population run proves (STANDARD via VENDOR quote):** the PCAOB's adopting release said "it was not possible to specify scenarios that would provide sufficient appropriate audit evidence" from TAA alone. — [MindBridge blog (Jul 2026)](https://www.mindbridge.ai/blog/pcaob-ai-audit-strategic-priorities/)
- **PCAOB inspection-driven failure modes for analytics (STANDARD/commentary):** imprecise expectations and missing investigation thresholds were the observed deficiencies behind the AS 2305 proposal. — [CPA Practice Advisor (Jun 2024)](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2024/06/13/pcaob-looks-to-clarify-rules-on-substantive-analytical-procedures/106774/)
- **Practitioner failure mode in deterministic tests:** an IDEA user forum shows a duplicate-JE test breaking on type-mixing (date, character and numeric fields concatenated). Deterministic tests fail silently when the data shape differs from what the test assumes. — [ideascripting.com forum](https://ideascripting.com/node/1205)
- **Vendor effectiveness claims (VENDOR, unverified):** "95%+ auto-certification on routine reconciliations" (BlackLine, via a comparison site). — [CFO Shortlist](https://www.cfoshortlist.com/reports/blackline-vs-floqast)

### Inferences
- Applied to the workpaper, the evidence favours **few, structural, high-precision gates** (status gates, input→output completeness, independent-expectation comparison with pre-set thresholds) over many low-precision anomaly detectors. A false-positive flood is a documented route to reviewers ignoring flags. That is also how a "not computed" admission can sit unread on screen.
- The Gold finding warns against treating "all checks green on our own engine's data" as assurance. Independent-route expectations (a second calculation path, prior-year relationships, external rate tables) are the analogue of the external industry data that auditors under-weighted.
- The IDEA type-mixing example supports the global rule "a zero must be proven possible to be non-zero." Each deterministic check should have a test showing it fires on a seeded positive. The project's per-mistake tests already do this; extending them to each structural gate would close the loop.

### Gaps
- I found no independent (non-vendor) field study quantifying detection rates or false-positive rates for MindBridge, IDEA, DataSnipper, BlackLine or FloQast.
- The journal venue and final findings of the Barr-Pulliam et al. and Gold et al. studies were not confirmed (source pages blocked or abstract-only).
- No *Journal of Accounting Research* article on audit anomaly detection was retrieved in this pass.
