## Q1. AICPA Statements on Standards for Tax Services (revised SSTS, effective 1 January 2024): which sections bear on the workpaper?

### Takeaway
The revised SSTSs (Nos. 1–4) are the AICPA's enforceable tax-practice standards for members. They contain **no explicit "workpaper" or "documentation" standard and no supervision/review standard**. They shape the workpaper indirectly: reliance on information and reasonable inquiry (2.3), use of prior-year returns (2.3.2, 2.3.9), estimates (2.4), unanswered return questions (2.2), positions and disclosure (1.1, 2.1), errors in prior returns (1.2), data protection (1.3), reliance on tools including AI (1.4), and documentation of advice "by professional judgment" (3.1.3).

### Cited Findings

**Status, structure and enforceability**
- The AICPA Tax Executive Committee adopted the revised SSTSs on 18 May 2023, effective 1 January 2024. They were reorganised by type of tax work and added three new standards: data protection, reliance on tools, and tax representation — [AICPA download page](https://www.aicpa-cima.com/resources/download/revised-statements-on-standards-for-tax-services-no-1-4-1-1-2024); [Journal of Accountancy](https://www.journalofaccountancy.com/news/2023/nov/aicpa-adopts-revised-tax-standards/)
- **Superseded:** the old SSTS Nos. 1–7 (in force before 1/1/2024) are replaced — [AICPA, SSTS 1–7 (prior to 1/1/2024)](https://www.aicpa-cima.com/resources/download/statements-on-standards-for-tax-services-no-1-7). A mapping document traces old to new paragraphs (e.g. old SSTS No. 3 "Certain Procedural Aspects" → new 2.3; old No. 4 → 2.4; old No. 6 "Knowledge of Error" → 1.2; old No. 1 → 2.1; old No. 7 "Form and Content of Advice" → 3.x) — [SSTS PDF, mapping appendix](https://bradfordtaxinstitute.com/Endnotes/AICPA_ssts_no_1_4.pdf)
- New structure: SSTS No. 1 General Standards (1.1 Advising on Tax Positions; 1.2 Knowledge of Errors; 1.3 Data Protection; 1.4 Reliance on Tools); SSTS No. 2 Tax Compliance incl. Tax Return Positions (2.1 Tax Return Positions; 2.2 Tax Return Questions; 2.3 Reliance on Information From Others; 2.4 Use of Estimates; 2.5 Departure From Previous Positions); SSTS No. 3 Tax Consulting (3.1.x); SSTS No. 4 Tax Representation (4.1.x) — [SSTS PDF](https://bradfordtaxinstitute.com/Endnotes/AICPA_ssts_no_1_4.pdf)
- Enforcement is through the AICPA Code's "General Standards Rule" (ET 1.300.001/2.300.001) and "Compliance With Standards Rule" (ET 1.310.001/2.310.001); "many state boards of accountancy also incorporate the SSTSs" into their rules. They apply to "all members providing tax services" (member as defined in ET 0.100) — [SSTS PDF, Introduction and Definitions](https://bradfordtaxinstitute.com/Endnotes/AICPA_ssts_no_1_4.pdf). The FAQ states the SSTSs are "enforceable tax practice standards for members of the AICPA" — [SSTS FAQ](https://assets.ctfassets.net/rb9cdnjh59cm/59x5bb0KdXzhm4TLCs4zBp/3d6e7a15cf5652ba1302d99af5d1aeda/faq-for-statements-on-standards-for-tax-services-ssts-effective_1-1-2024.pdf)
- Definition: a **tax position** is (i) a position on a return on which the member specifically advised, or (ii) a position about which the member knows all material facts and has concluded whether it is appropriate (Definitions); 1.1.4 adds it is "a conclusion reached when applicable tax law, regulations, case law, or other regulatory or recognized guidance is applied to a particular transaction, a specific set of facts and circumstances, or a controversy" — [SSTS PDF](https://bradfordtaxinstitute.com/Endnotes/AICPA_ssts_no_1_4.pdf)
  - Implication for the tool: model "positions" as first-class objects (the conclusion, the facts it rests on, the authority cited), separate from the numbers they produce.

**1.1 Advising on Tax Positions / 2.1 Tax Return Positions (standards for positions and disclosure)**
- 2.1.6: comply with the taxing authority's standards; if none, do not prepare/sign unless there is a good-faith belief the position has at least a **realistic possibility** of being sustained; if the authority's standard is higher, meet it; a member may sign a return with a position that has a **reasonable basis** if it is **appropriately disclosed**. 1.1.5 mirrors this for advice — [SSTS PDF](https://bradfordtaxinstitute.com/Endnotes/AICPA_ssts_no_1_4.pdf)
- 2.1.7 / 2.1.15 / 1.1.12: advise the taxpayer of potential penalty consequences and the opportunity to avoid them by disclosure; the taxpayer decides whether to disclose; if the member believes disclosure is required and the taxpayer refuses, consider whether to continue the relationship — [SSTS PDF](https://bradfordtaxinstitute.com/Endnotes/AICPA_ssts_no_1_4.pdf)
- 2.1.8: do not take a position the member knows exploits the audit selection process or is a pure negotiating position — [SSTS PDF](https://bradfordtaxinstitute.com/Endnotes/AICPA_ssts_no_1_4.pdf)
- 2.1.9: may rely in good faith on others' proposed positions if 2.3 is satisfied — [SSTS PDF](https://bradfordtaxinstitute.com/Endnotes/AICPA_ssts_no_1_4.pdf)
- 2.1.11: "A tax return is prepared based on a taxpayer's representation of facts, and the taxpayer has the final responsibility for positions taken on the return" — [SSTS PDF](https://bradfordtaxinstitute.com/Endnotes/AICPA_ssts_no_1_4.pdf)
- FAQ example: a member who concludes a position has reasonable basis but not substantial authority tells the client Form 8275 is required and "prepares a memorandum for her files documenting this communication" — [SSTS FAQ](https://assets.ctfassets.net/rb9cdnjh59cm/59x5bb0KdXzhm4TLCs4zBp/3d6e7a15cf5652ba1302d99af5d1aeda/faq-for-statements-on-standards-for-tax-services-ssts-effective_1-1-2024.pdf)
  - Implication for the tool: each judgement-based position needs a support-level field (substantial authority / reasonable basis + disclosed / realistic possibility), a disclosure flag (Form 8275), and a record of the penalty advice given to the client.

**1.2 Knowledge of Errors (errors in prior returns)**
- 1.2.2: "error" = any position, omission or accounting method that failed the 1.1/2.1 standards when taken, including prior-year positions made wrong by retroactive law; excludes items with an "insignificant effect" (judged per 1.2.16). 1.2.3: applies whether or not the member prepared the erroneous return — [SSTS PDF](https://bradfordtaxinstitute.com/Endnotes/AICPA_ssts_no_1_4.pdf)
- 1.2.6: "promptly inform" the taxpayer of a failure to file, an error in a previously filed return, etc.; advise on consequences and corrective measures. "Such advice may be given orally. See also paragraph 3.1.3 regarding the documentation of advice" — [SSTS PDF](https://bradfordtaxinstitute.com/Endnotes/AICPA_ssts_no_1_4.pdf)
- 1.2.7 / 1.2.15: if the taxpayer does not correct a prior-year error, consider withdrawing; if the member prepares the current return, "take reasonable steps to ensure that the error is not repeated" — [SSTS PDF](https://bradfordtaxinstitute.com/Endnotes/AICPA_ssts_no_1_4.pdf)
- 1.2.8: the member may not tell the taxing authority without the taxpayer's permission unless law requires; 1.2.9: possible fraud → advise the taxpayer to consult an attorney — [SSTS PDF](https://bradfordtaxinstitute.com/Endnotes/AICPA_ssts_no_1_4.pdf)
  - Implication for the tool: a prior-year finding needs its own record type: what the error is, its materiality judgement, date the client was told, the client's decision, and a carry-forward guard so the error is not repeated in the current year.

**1.3 Data Protection (new)**
- 1.3.4: "make reasonable efforts to safeguard taxpayer data, including data transmitted or stored electronically"; 1.3.5: consider applicable privacy laws. No bright-line rules (1.3.1) — [SSTS PDF](https://bradfordtaxinstitute.com/Endnotes/AICPA_ssts_no_1_4.pdf)
- 1.3.7–1.3.13: safeguards may include security software, encryption in transit, secure networks, strong passwords, firewalls, secure sharing platforms; consider the AICPA Privacy Management Framework and Trust Services Criteria; confirm third-party hosted tools protect data (1.3.8); **limit confidential data held** — request only what is needed, delete/return/redact unneeded data, ask clients to mask PII/PHI, follow retention-and-destruction policies (1.3.9); plan for breaches (1.3.10); GLBA/FTC Safeguards Rule requires a **written information security plan** (1.3.11); train supervised non-member staff (1.3.13) — [SSTS PDF](https://bradfordtaxinstitute.com/Endnotes/AICPA_ssts_no_1_4.pdf)
  - Implication for the tool: data minimisation (mask SSNs by default, redact unneeded fields), retention-driven deletion, encryption and access control are standards-backed features, not polish.

**1.4 Reliance on Tools (new)**
- 1.4.2: tools include tax preparation software, research publications, calculation aids, data analytics, statistical models, **artificial intelligence**. 1.4.3–1.4.4: exercise professional judgment and care; may reasonably rely on tools, but "use of a tool does not absolve the member of professional obligations" — [SSTS PDF](https://bradfordtaxinstitute.com/Endnotes/AICPA_ssts_no_1_4.pdf)
- 1.4.7: the member "remains responsible for the completed work product" and should take reasonable steps to determine tools are appropriate. 1.4.8: tools must not "supplant the member's professional judgment"; the Form 1040 perjury attestation "cannot be transferred entirely to reliance on a tool" — [SSTS PDF](https://bradfordtaxinstitute.com/Endnotes/AICPA_ssts_no_1_4.pdf)
- A secondary summary adds that it "would generally not be reasonable" to assume a return produced by standard software was complete without reviewing it — [Washington Society of CPAs](https://www.wscpa.org/blog/49e3268e-2b98-4079-a4e5-2be39f1bfcf4:navigating-the-revised-statements-on-standards-for-tax-services) (exact wording not located in the standard text itself; may come from the FAQ or interpretation — **unverified as SSTS text**)
  - Implication for the tool: computed and extracted values (including any OCR/AI extraction) must show which tool produced them and require a human sign-off; the tool assists, the preparer attests.

**2.2 Tax Return Questions**
- 2.2.2: before signing, "take reasonable steps to obtain from the taxpayer the information necessary to provide appropriate answers to all required questions on a tax return". 2.2.5: reasonable grounds to omit include immaterial unavailable info, genuine uncertainty, voluminous info (state it will be supplied on request). 2.2.6: do not omit merely because the answer is disadvantageous — [SSTS PDF](https://bradfordtaxinstitute.com/Endnotes/AICPA_ssts_no_1_4.pdf)
  - Implication for the tool: track every return question (e.g. digital-asset question, foreign accounts) with answer, source and, if blank, the reason.

**2.3 Reliance on Information From Others (reasonable inquiry; prior-year returns)**
- 2.3.2: a member "may, in good faith, rely on information furnished, without verification, by the taxpayer or by third parties. However, a member should not ignore the implications of information furnished and should make reasonable inquiries if the information furnished appears to be incorrect, incomplete, or inconsistent either on its face or on the basis of other facts known to the member. A member should consider one or more of the taxpayer's prior-year tax returns, or a portion of a return, whenever feasible" — [SSTS PDF](https://bradfordtaxinstitute.com/Endnotes/AICPA_ssts_no_1_4.pdf)
- 2.3.3: where law imposes a condition on deductibility (e.g. substantiation), make reasonable inquiries to determine "to the member's satisfaction" that it is met. 2.3.4: consider relevant information actually known from other sources, including another taxpayer's return, subject to confidentiality limits — [SSTS PDF](https://bradfordtaxinstitute.com/Endnotes/AICPA_ssts_no_1_4.pdf)
- 2.3.6–2.3.7: no requirement to examine underlying documentation; unsupported client lists (dividends, interest, charity, medical) may be used unless they appear incorrect/incomplete/inconsistent; but the member should encourage clients to provide supporting data, e.g. for securities transactions and pass-through entities. 2.3.8: K-1 information may be accepted without inquiry unless there is reason to doubt it — [SSTS PDF](https://bradfordtaxinstitute.com/Endnotes/AICPA_ssts_no_1_4.pdf)
- 2.3.9: use prior-year returns "whenever feasible" to determine general tax status, "avoid the omission or duplication of items", and give a basis for consistent treatment; extent of comparison depends on circumstances — [SSTS PDF](https://bradfordtaxinstitute.com/Endnotes/AICPA_ssts_no_1_4.pdf)
- FAQ: a new preparer may rely on a prior preparer's software "Shareholder Basis Worksheet" if it appears reasonable after reviewing prior returns and confirming the prior preparer's competence; where a firm destroyed returns and working papers after 7 years, it may rely on the worksheet "if the numbers seem reasonable after examining the seven most recent returns" — [SSTS FAQ](https://assets.ctfassets.net/rb9cdnjh59cm/59x5bb0KdXzhm4TLCs4zBp/3d6e7a15cf5652ba1302d99af5d1aeda/faq-for-statements-on-standards-for-tax-services-ssts-effective_1-1-2024.pdf)
  - Implication for the tool: this is the core of the workpaper. Every value needs its source (client, third party, prior year, computed), automated consistency checks (face, cross-document, prior-year), and an inquiry log (question asked, answer, date, who) whenever a check fires.

**2.4 Use of Estimates**
- 2.4.2: may use taxpayer estimates if exact data is impractical to obtain and the estimates are reasonable; "Estimates should be presented in a manner that does not imply greater accuracy than exists." 2.4.5: inform the taxpayer of its duty to keep supporting records. 2.4.7: disclose estimates in unusual cases (death/illness, missing K-1, pending litigation, destroyed records). Appraisals are not estimates (2.4.1) — [SSTS PDF](https://bradfordtaxinstitute.com/Endnotes/AICPA_ssts_no_1_4.pdf)
  - Implication for the tool: an "estimate" flag on a value, with basis and whether disclosure is needed, distinct from document-sourced values.

**2.5 Departure From Previous Positions**
- 2.5.4: may depart from treatment concluded in a prior exam/appeal/court decision (unless bound) if 2.1 is met; prior consent or unfavourable decision is a factor (2.5.6) — [SSTS PDF](https://bradfordtaxinstitute.com/Endnotes/AICPA_ssts_no_1_4.pdf)
  - Implication for the tool: year-over-year treatment changes should be flagged and annotated.

**3.1 Tax Consulting — documentation of advice**
- 3.1.3: advice may be oral or written; written advice must meet taxing-authority standards (Circular 230 §10.37); "A member should use professional judgment about any need to document oral advice. A member is not required to follow a standard format." 3.1.7: written communication recommended for important, unusual, high-dollar or complicated transactions — [SSTS PDF](https://bradfordtaxinstitute.com/Endnotes/AICPA_ssts_no_1_4.pdf)
- FAQ: where the client asks for oral advice only, "it is recommended that the member contemporaneously document the advice in written form in the taxpayer's file" — [SSTS FAQ](https://assets.ctfassets.net/rb9cdnjh59cm/59x5bb0KdXzhm4TLCs4zBp/3d6e7a15cf5652ba1302d99af5d1aeda/faq-for-statements-on-standards-for-tax-services-ssts-effective_1-1-2024.pdf)
  - Implication for the tool: a timestamped client-communication note attached to the item it concerns.

### Inferences
- "Quality control/supervision" is **not** a section of the revised SSTSs; supervision duties for tax come from Circular 230 §10.22(b) and §10.36, the §6694 firm-penalty rules, and (for A&A firms only) SQMS No. 1. The only supervision-like SSTS text is 1.3.13 (train supervised staff on data protection).
- SSTS documentation duties are judgement-based ("should consider", "professional judgment"), so a tool that captures sources, inquiries and communications by default produces the evidence a member would need to show compliance.

### Gaps
- I did not read the AICPA's separate SSTS interpretations (if any were reissued under the revised framework) or the paid SSTS Library; any 2025–2026 amendments to the SSTSs were not found in searches (none surfaced) — **unverified that no amendments exist as of Oct 2026**.

## Q2. Is there an authoritative US "tax workpaper standard"? What do AICPA quality-management standards say, and does SQMS No. 1 apply to tax-only practices?

### Takeaway
No. Unlike audit documentation (AU-C 230, PCAOB AS 1215), there is **no authoritative US standard that prescribes the content of tax workpapers**. Tax documentation is governed indirectly: by the SSTSs (Q1), Circular 230 (Q3), statutory preparer-record rules (Q4), penalty-defence needs (§6694 reasonable cause, contemporaneous documentation), and state board rules on records (Q6). SQMS No. 1 covers only a firm's accounting-and-auditing practice, so it does not by itself reach tax engagements.

### Cited Findings
- SQMS No. 1 (issued by the AICPA Auditing Standards Board, June 2022) is effective **15 December 2025**; it supersedes SQCS No. 8 — [Linford & Co.](https://linfordco.com/blog/aicpa-quality-management-standards-sqms-1/); [Thomson Reuters](https://tax.thomsonreuters.com/blog/sqms-1-explained-a-practical-guide-to-firm-level-quality-management/)
- Its objective is a system of quality management "for engagements performed by the firm in its accounting and auditing practice", defined as audit, attestation, review and compilation (and preparation) engagements and other services named by the ASB or ARSC — [Linford & Co.](https://linfordco.com/blog/aicpa-quality-management-standards-sqms-1/); [Oregon Society of CPAs](https://www.orcpa.org/news/6c89ad9b-b4f2-46c9-8d13-1314235fe5f8:new-standards-for-quality-management-in-your-accounting-or-auditing-practice)
- For AICPA Peer Review, an "accounting and auditing practice" means engagements under SASs, SSARSs, SSAEs, Government Auditing Standards and PCAOB standards; peer review "does not cover tax, consulting, investment advisory or other services" — [FICPA](https://ficpa.org/storage/files/c9ccc2b97159060b4dba3410f740d578.pdf); [Washington Society of CPAs](https://www.wscpa.org/blog/article/wscpa-blog/2018/05/16/q-a-enrolling-in-the-peer-review-program)
- State examples: New York requires peer review for firms performing any attest services; Florida exempts firms with no audit/attest; Tennessee requires it only for attest firms — [NYSED](https://www.op.nysed.gov/professions/certified-public-accountants/mandatory-peer-review); [Tennessee](https://support.commerce.tn.gov/hc/en-us/articles/209637237-Peer-Review-FAQs)
- AICPA Code ET 1.400.200 Records Requests (effective 31 July 2021): client-provided records may not be withheld; member-prepared records and work product must be provided on request but may be withheld for unpaid fees; "the member is not required to provide the client with their working papers." AICPA FAQs treat the **tax software data file as a working paper** — [The Tax Adviser, Feb 2023](https://www.thetaxadviser.com/issues/2023/feb/documentation-and-recordkeeping-for-tax-practitioners)
- The Tax Adviser's best-practice list of records to retain: deliverables, client communications, supporting workpapers, carryforward calculations, research, executed engagement letters describing scope, evidence of delivery; return client originals rather than keep them; generally do not keep drafts not given to the client; file service-related emails with the engagement and delete from email; a **written document-retention policy** applied equally to paper and electronic records, reviewed annually with secure destruction — [The Tax Adviser, Feb 2023](https://www.thetaxadviser.com/issues/2023/feb/documentation-and-recordkeeping-for-tax-practitioners)
- The SSTSs themselves say no standard format for documenting advice can be established (3.1.6) — [SSTS PDF](https://bradfordtaxinstitute.com/Endnotes/AICPA_ssts_no_1_4.pdf)
- Implication for the tool: the workpaper's structure is a product decision, not a compliance template; design it to produce the evidence the indirect rules demand (sources, inquiries, positions, review sign-offs, retention dates).

### Inferences
- A tax-only CPA firm is generally outside SQMS No. 1 and AICPA peer review (inference from the A&A definitions; no AICPA text found stating "tax-only firms are exempt" verbatim). A mixed firm's QM system need not extend to tax engagements, though many firms apply similar review practices voluntarily.
- Firm-level review discipline for tax therefore rests on Circular 230 §10.36 and the §6694 firm-penalty/"normal office practice" factors (Q3, Q4).

## Q3. Treasury Circular 230 (31 CFR Part 10)

### Takeaway
Circular 230 governs everyone who practises before the IRS (CPAs, attorneys, enrolled agents, and others). For workpapers it imposes: due diligence on accuracy (§10.22), good-faith reliance with reasonable inquiry (§10.34(d)), advising clients of errors (§10.21) and of penalties (§10.34(c)), competence (§10.35), written-advice standards (§10.37), return of client records (§10.28), and a firm-level duty for the person in charge to keep compliance procedures in place (§10.36).

### Cited Findings
- (Primary source eCFR redirected to a block page; Cornell LII mirror of the CFR used.)
- **§10.21 Knowledge of client's omission:** a practitioner who knows the client has not complied with revenue laws or made an "error in or omission from any return" must "advise the client promptly" and explain the consequences — [31 CFR 10.21](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/10.21)
  - Implication for the tool: same as SSTS 1.2 — record discovery date and date/channel of client notification.
- **§10.22 Diligence as to accuracy:** (a) due diligence in preparing, approving and filing returns and in determining correctness of statements to Treasury and to clients; (b) a practitioner is "presumed to have exercised due diligence" when relying on another person's work if they "used reasonable care in engaging, supervising, training, and evaluating the person" — [31 CFR 10.22](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/10.22)
  - Implication for the tool: the preparer/reviewer split must be recorded (who prepared, who reviewed, what they checked) — the reviewer's evidence of supervision is what earns the presumption.
- **§10.28 Return of client's records:** promptly return "any and all records of the client" on request (may keep copies); a fee dispute generally does not excuse this, but if state law allows retention, return at least records that must be attached to the return. "Records of the client" include pre-existing documents and third-party documents; excluded are practitioner-prepared returns, schedules, etc. withheld pending payment for them — [31 CFR 10.28](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/10.28)
  - Implication for the tool: tag every artefact as client-provided vs firm-prepared, so a "return client records" export is one click.
- **§10.34(a):** may not willfully, recklessly or through gross incompetence sign a return with a position lacking a reasonable basis, an "unreasonable position" (as defined by §6694), or a willful/reckless understatement. **(c):** must advise the client of penalties "reasonably likely to apply" and of disclosure-based penalty avoidance. **(d):** "may rely in good faith without verification upon information furnished by the client", but "may not ignore the implications of information furnished" and must make reasonable inquiries if it appears incorrect, inconsistent or incomplete (paraphrase of the fetched text) — [31 CFR 10.34](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/10.34)
  - Implication for the tool: inconsistency checks are the operational form of §10.34(d); each fired check should end in either a documented inquiry or a documented reason it is not a problem.
- **§10.35 Competence:** "Competent practice requires the appropriate level of knowledge, skill, thoroughness, and preparation"; competence may be gained by consulting experts — [31 CFR 10.35](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/10.35)
- **§10.36 Procedures to ensure compliance:** the individual(s) with "principal authority and responsibility" for the firm's tax practice must take "reasonable steps to ensure that the firm has adequate procedures in effect" for compliance with Part 10; discipline applies (willfully, recklessly, or through gross incompetence) for failing to have or enforce procedures where a pattern of noncompliance occurs, or for not promptly correcting a known pattern — [31 CFR 10.36](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/10.36)
  - Implication for the tool: firm-level review rules (required sign-offs, checklists) configured once and enforced on every return give the responsible partner evidence of "adequate procedures".
- **§10.37 Written advice:** must rest on reasonable factual and legal assumptions; reasonably consider all relevant facts the practitioner knows or should know; use reasonable efforts to identify and ascertain facts; not rely unreasonably on representations; relate law to facts; not consider the chance of audit. Reliance on others' advice only if reasonable and in good faith. Judged by a "reasonable practitioner standard" — [31 CFR 10.37](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/10.37)
- **Superseded note:** the former "covered opinion" rules of §10.35 were removed when Circular 230 was amended in 2014 and replaced by the current §10.35 competence and §10.37 rules — **unverified** in this session (from background knowledge; not re-fetched).

### Inferences
- Circular 230 reliance language (§10.34(d)) and SSTS 2.3.2 are almost identical; the tool can implement one "reliance + inquiry" model that satisfies both.

### Gaps
- §10.29 (conflicts), §10.30, §10.50/§10.51 sanctions not reviewed (outside workpaper scope). §10.28 does not itself set any retention period.

## Q4. IRC and regulations on preparers: §6107, §6695 (incl. (g) and Form 8867 / Treas. Reg. §1.6695-2), §6694, §7216, §6060

### Takeaway
Statute requires surprisingly little retention: a copy or list of returns for 3 years (§6107), a list of employed preparers for 3 years (§6060), and — for EITC/CTC/ACTC/ODC/AOTC/head of household — Form 8867 plus computation records, inquiry notes and copies of relied-on documents for 3 years (§1.6695-2). §6694 drives the evidence a preparer needs to defend positions, including a **contemporaneous documentation** rule for penalty advice. §7216 restricts use/disclosure of return information.

### Cited Findings

**§6107 Copy or list of returns**
- (a) give the taxpayer a completed copy no later than when presented for signature; (b) for 3 years after the close of the "return period", keep a completed copy **or** a list of taxpayer name and TIN, available to the IRS on request — [26 USC 6107](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/6107)
- Reg: keep a copy, or a record of name, TIN, taxable year and type of return, plus the name of the individual preparer required to sign; 3 years after close of the return period in which presented for signature — [26 CFR 1.6107-1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.6107-1)
- "Return period" (§6060(c)) is the 12-month period beginning 1 July — [26 CFR 1.6060-1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.6060-1); [The Tax Adviser](https://www.thetaxadviser.com/issues/2023/feb/documentation-and-recordkeeping-for-tax-practitioners)
  - Implication for the tool: store the signed return, preparer of record and presentation date; compute a minimum retention date from them.

**§6060 Information returns of preparers**
- An employer of signing preparers must keep a record of each preparer's name, TIN and principal place of work for 3 years after the close of the return period — [26 CFR 1.6060-1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.6060-1)
  - Implication for the tool: user records should hold preparer identity (PTIN) and office, with history.

**§6695 Other preparer penalties**
- (a) failure to furnish copy, (b) failure to sign, (c) failure to furnish identifying number (PTIN), (d) failure to retain copy or list, (e) failure to file correct §6060 info: each $50 base, capped at $25,000 per year/return period; (f) negotiating a client's refund check $500; (g) due-diligence failure for head of household, CTC/ACTC (§24), AOTC (§25A), EITC (§32): $500 base per failure; (h) inflation-indexed for returns filed after 2014 — [26 USC 6695](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/6695)
- IRS: "For returns and refunds filed in 2026, the penalty is $650 per failure to be diligent" — [IRS, News and updates for paid preparers](https://www.irs.gov/node/146356). A secondary study guide gives $635 for 2025 returns filed in 2026 — [open-exam-prep](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/ea/tax-return-basics/preparer-due-diligence); conflicts with the IRS figure; prefer IRS ($650). Rev. Proc. number not located.

**Treas. Reg. §1.6695-2 (due diligence; Form 8867)**
- (b)(1) complete Form 8867 (Paid Preparer's Due Diligence Checklist) from information provided by or reasonably obtained from the taxpayer; e-filing signer files it with the return; non-signing preparer gives it to the signer — [26 CFR 1.6695-2](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.6695-2)
- (b)(2) complete the IRS worksheet for the credit or keep your own record of the computation, including method and information used — [26 CFR 1.6695-2](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.6695-2)
- (b)(3) knowledge requirement: must not know or have reason to know information is incorrect; if a reasonable, well-informed preparer would find it incorrect, inconsistent or incomplete, make reasonable inquiries, and **"contemporaneously document"** the inquiries and responses. Example: a Form 1098-T alone does not establish AOTC eligibility — [26 CFR 1.6695-2](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.6695-2)
- (b)(4) retention: keep (i) a copy of Form 8867, each completed worksheet or computation record, a record of how and when the information was obtained including the identity of who provided it, and copies of taxpayer documents relied on; (ii) for **3 years** from the latest of the return due date (without extensions), the e-file date, the date presented for signature (paper), or the date a non-signer gave its part to the signer; (iii) paper or electronic — [26 CFR 1.6695-2](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.6695-2)
- (d) exception: normal office procedures reasonably designed and routinely followed, and failure isolated and inadvertent — [26 CFR 1.6695-2](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.6695-2)
- Max per return if all four benefits claimed: $2,600 (2026) — [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/node/146356)
  - Implication for the tool: the Form 8867 rule is the closest thing US law has to a workpaper spec — "what was asked, what was answered, who said it, when, and which document was relied on". Build every inquiry log to that shape, not only for these credits.

**§6694 Understatement penalties**
- (a) unreasonable position: penalty if the preparer knew or should have known of the position and it lacked **substantial authority** (undisclosed), **reasonable basis** (disclosed), or **more-likely-than-not** (tax shelter/reportable transaction); reasonable cause and good faith exception; greater of $1,000 or 50% of income derived. (b) willful or reckless: greater of $5,000 or 75% — [26 USC 6694](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/6694)
- §1.6694-1(e): preparer may rely in good faith without verification on taxpayer information but must make reasonable inquiries if it appears incorrect or incomplete, and appropriate inquiries where the Code requires specific facts (example: $50,000 real estate donation without asking about appraisal/Form 8283 → penalty) — [26 CFR 1.6694-1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.6694-1)
- §1.6694-1(b): the signing preparer is generally primarily responsible for all positions; only one individual per firm is primarily responsible per position; the firm can also be penalised — [26 CFR 1.6694-1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.6694-1)
- §1.6694-2(d)(3): where a preparer relies on advising the taxpayer of penalty standards/disclosure, "The tax return preparer must also contemporaneously document the advice in the tax return preparer's files"; "A general disclaimer will not satisfy the requirement"; advice must be particular to the taxpayer — [26 CFR 1.6694-2](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.6694-2)
- §1.6694-2(e) reasonable-cause factors: nature of the error, frequency, materiality, **normal office practice**, reliance on others' advice, reliance on generally accepted practice. §1.6694-2(a) firm penalty arises in limited cases involving principal management, review procedures, or disregard of those procedures — [26 CFR 1.6694-2](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.6694-2)
  - Implication for the tool: per-position authority level, disclosure, and a dated client-specific penalty-advice note; a consistent review procedure is itself penalty defence.

**§7216 Disclosure and use of return information**
- §301.7216-2 permits without consent: use/disclosure within the same firm in the US to prepare the return (c)(2); to other US preparers for auxiliary non-substantive services and to e-file providers (d)(1); to software/equipment contractors limited to need, with written notice of §§6713/7216 (d)(2); **offshore access to US-stored data counts as disclosure outside the US and needs consent**, and SSNs may not be disclosed offshore by consent (example); retention of copies and return information in paper or electronic form (m); **quality or peer reviews** by attorneys, CPAs, EAs or enrolled actuaries only, data used only for the review, reviewed preparer keeps a record of the review, reviewer keeps no taxpayer-identifying documents (p) — [26 CFR 301.7216-2](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/301.7216-2)
- Consent form/content requirements are in §301.7216-3 (not fetched — **unverified** detail).
  - Implication for the tool: hosting location and who can view data (including offshore staff or AI vendors) is a §7216 question; log access and keep data in the US unless consent is captured.

**E-file records (Pub 1345)**
- EROs keep Forms 8453, 8879 and 8878 for three years, plus the acknowledgment file — [The Tax Adviser](https://www.thetaxadviser.com/issues/2023/feb/documentation-and-recordkeeping-for-tax-practitioners)
  - Implication for the tool: store signed e-file authorisations and IRS acknowledgments with the return version they authorise.

### Inferences
- Federal statutory minimums are all 3 years; the longer firm retention (7 years) is driven by statute-of-limitations exposure and penalty defence (Q5), not by a statutory preparer-retention rule.

## Q5. Statute of limitations (§6501) and why firms keep workpapers 7+ years

### Takeaway
Assessment is generally limited to 3 years from filing, 6 years for a >25% gross-income omission, and unlimited for fraud or non-filing. Firms commonly keep tax workpapers about 7 years to cover the 6-year window plus margin and the 7-year refund period for bad debts/worthless securities; no federal rule mandates 7 years for tax workpapers.

### Cited Findings
- §6501(a): assessment within 3 years after the return was filed; (b)(1) an early return is treated as filed on the due date — [26 USC 6501](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/6501)
- §6501(c)(1)–(3): false/fraudulent return with intent to evade, willful attempt to evade, or no return → assessment "at any time"; (c)(8) unreported foreign transfer information keeps the period open until 3 years after it is supplied — [26 USC 6501](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/6501)
- §6501(e)(1)(A): omission of more than 25% of gross income stated in the return → 6 years; separate 6-year rule for specified foreign-asset income over $5,000 — [26 USC 6501](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/6501)
- §6511(d)(1): 7 years for refund claims on bad debts/worthless securities; "A commonly mentioned retention period for tax practitioners is seven years" (citing an AICPA resource); retention need not match limitation periods but longer retention helps clients; state limitation periods also matter — [The Tax Adviser](https://www.thetaxadviser.com/issues/2023/feb/documentation-and-recordkeeping-for-tax-practitioners)
- §6112 material advisers keep lists for 7 years — [The Tax Adviser](https://www.thetaxadviser.com/issues/2023/feb/documentation-and-recordkeeping-for-tax-practitioners)
- SSTS 1.3.9 ties deletion to "the respective statute of limitations or the member's document retention policies" — [SSTS PDF](https://bradfordtaxinstitute.com/Endnotes/AICPA_ssts_no_1_4.pdf)
- Implication for the tool: store filing date per return version and compute retention dates (3/6/7 years, open-ended if unfiled or flagged), with a legal-hold override.

### Inferences
- Carryforwards (capital losses, basis, NOLs) mean a prior-year workpaper can remain relevant long past 7 years (consistent with the FAQ basis-worksheet example in Q1); the tool should let carryforward schedules outlive the general retention policy.

### Gaps
- No AICPA primary document stating "seven years" was retrieved.

## Q6. State boards and state preparer rules (California, Texas)

### Takeaway
State accountancy boards mostly regulate **ownership and return of records** and set retention periods for **attest** work (California 7 years for audit documentation; Texas 5 years for attest work papers), not tax workpapers. California's non-CPA preparer statute requires a 4-year copy of each return — longer than the federal 3 years.

### Cited Findings

**California**
- 16 CCR §68: after a client's demand, a licensee "shall not retain" the client's books and records; unpaid fees do not justify retention; working papers are the licensee's property, but information in them that would ordinarily be part of the client's books must be treated as client records — [Justia, 16 CCR 68](https://regulations.justia.com/states/california/title-16/division-1/article-9/section-68) (text via search snippet; direct fetch blocked — wording **not fully verified**)
- 16 CCR §68.1: defines working papers; licensees must retain them for a period sufficient to meet practice needs and legal requirements, and must keep them during any pending Board investigation, disciplinary or legal action until notified in writing — [Justia, 16 CCR 68.1](https://regulations.justia.com/states/california/title-16/division-1/article-9/section-68-1/) (via search snippet — **not fully verified**)
- 16 CCR §68.3 and §68.5: audit documentation retention measured from report release date under Bus. & Prof. Code §5097 (7 years — figure **unverified** from statute text), with a required written Audit Documentation Retention and Destruction Policy — [Justia, 16 CCR 68.3](https://regulations.justia.com/states/california/title-16/division-1/article-9/section-68-3/); [Justia, 16 CCR 68.5](https://regulations.justia.com/states/california/title-16/division-1/article-9/section-68-5/). These apply to audits, not tax.
- Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code §22253(a)(5): it is a violation for a tax preparer to "fail to maintain a copy of any tax return prepared for a customer for four years from the date of completion or the due date of the return, whichever is later"; also (a)(4) give customers copies of documents they sign, (a)(8) violating 26 USC §7216, (a)(10) failing to return customer records on demand — [California Public Law, BPC 22253](https://california.public.law/codes/business_and_professions_code_section_22253)
- CTEC registers non-exempt paid preparers; CPAs licensed by the CBA, attorneys and enrolled agents are exempt from CTEC registration — [CTEC code of conduct](https://ctec.org/tax-professionals/preparer-code-of-conduct); [Justia BPC 22253 listing](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-bpc/division-8/chapter-14/section-22253) (exemption detail via search snippet — whether the §22253 four-year rule binds exempt CPAs is **unverified**)
  - Implication for the tool: make the retention floor configurable by state (CA: 4 years for return copies) and support "legal hold" when a board matter is open.

**Texas**
- 22 TAC §501.76(a)(1): return original client records "promptly, not to exceed 10 business days" after request, no fee; (a)(2)–(3): work papers that contain records ordinarily part of the client's books and not otherwise available — e.g. ledgers substitutes, depreciation records, adjusting entries, **consolidating entries and support used to reach final figures in tax returns** — must be furnished within 20 business days (reasonable fee allowed); (b) other work papers are the licensee's property; (c) on request, provide a copy of the client's tax return for a reasonable charge; (e) attest documentation kept "not less than five years from the date of any report issued"; (g) client records include client files from commercial tax software and the passwords to open them — [Cornell LII, 22 Tex. Admin. Code §501.76](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/texas/22-Tex-Admin-Code-SS-501-76)
- TSBPA FAQ: the Board "has not established record retention guidelines for other services" (i.e. non-attest, including tax) — [TSBPA enforcement FAQ](https://www.tsbpa.texas.gov/enforcement/faq.html) (via search snippet)
  - Implication for the tool: a client-records export must include the tax software data in a usable format and the supporting schedules behind return figures; Texas sets a 10/20 business-day clock.

**California FTB preparer rules**
- No FTB-specific preparer workpaper or due-diligence documentation rule was found in this session (California has its own EITC, but an FTB due-diligence documentation rule was not located) — **gap**.

### Inferences
- The client-records vs work-papers line (federal §10.28, AICPA ET 1.400.200, CA §68, TX §501.76) is consistent: client-supplied and "books-like" schedules belong to the client; analysis and judgement belong to the firm. The tool should label each artefact accordingly.

- CA 16 CCR §68/68.1 and TSBPA FAQ text came from search snippets (direct fetch returned 403); confirm against the official CCR/TAC.
- California FTB preparer due-diligence or record requirements (e.g. for CalEITC) not found.
- Other states (e.g. New York's 7216-style rules) not researched.

## Cross-cutting: data security law (GLBA / FTC Safeguards Rule)

### Takeaway
Tax preparers are "financial institutions" for GLBA purposes and must keep a written information security plan (WISP); the IRS's Publication 5708 is a template. SSTS 1.3.11 explicitly cites this.

### Cited Findings
- SSTS 1.3.11: GLBA requires preparers to ensure security and confidentiality of customer financial information; the FTC Safeguards Rule requires a written information security plan and oversight of service providers — [SSTS PDF](https://bradfordtaxinstitute.com/Endnotes/AICPA_ssts_no_1_4.pdf)
- IRS: tax professionals are required to have a written information security plan; Pub 5708 (Rev. 8-2024) is a template prepared by the Security Summit — [IRS newsroom](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-security-summit-remind-tax-pros-they-need-a-written-information-security-plan-to-protect-client-data); [CPA Practice Advisor](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2024/08/14/irs-updates-wisp-guide-for-tax-professionals/109001/)
- Safeguards Rule: designate a qualified individual; risk assessment; safeguards monitored and tested; vendor oversight; report security events affecting 500+ people to the FTC, generally within 30 days — [NATP](https://www.natptax.com/news-insights/blog/why-your-tax-practice-needs-a-written-information-security-plan/); [CPA Practice Advisor 2025](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2025/07/29/irs-reminds-tax-pros-of-requirement-for-a-written-security-plan-wisp/165822/) (MFA wording **unverified** against the rule text)
- Implication for the tool: role-based access, MFA, encryption, audit logging of who viewed what, and vendor (including AI/LLM provider) controls are expected of any system that stores client tax data.

### Gaps
- 16 CFR Part 314 (Safeguards Rule) text not fetched directly.
