# Make every line prove its own derivation

Research synthesised Sat 24 Oct 2026 from five research notes gathered the same day. Vendor figures are marked **[vendor claim]**. Cost figures are the writer's rough estimates for this codebase, not sourced numbers.

Nobody catches unprecedented errors by writing a cleverer detector. Across tax software, audit analytics, data-quality tools and spreadsheet auditors, the errors nobody wrote a rule for get caught by **closed-world rules**: every line, account or cell must carry a derivation, a reconciliation or a source link, and anything without one is an open item by construction. The IRS adds independent recomputation, third-party matching and statistical scoring on top. The California AMT miss fits this pattern exactly: a filed line held at 0 while a 40,000 input pointed at it, with the engine admitting it never computed the line. A coverage gate catches that without knowing anything about AMT, and CHK-LINE-NOT-COMPUTED (shipped 24 Oct) is the first version of that gate. **Recommendation:** between the 15 known-mistake check families and the AI reader, add a **structural layer** in this order. (1) Generalise the line-status gate. (2) Require every non-zero input to reach a filed line in every jurisdiction in scope, or to carry a stated disposition. (3) Use the packet's three return versions as natural experiments: when an input changed and a line that depends on it did not move, flag it. (4) Compare a few high-value lines against expectations computed by an independent route, with thresholds fixed in advance. A second open-source engine comes later. The AI layer should become agentic, because the evidence for that is strong: one blind tool-using run found 22 of 27 mistakes, against 13 for the per-document reader. But it should run **in parallel** as a lead generator, and every rule it writes should pass a deterministic promotion gate before it can block anything.

## Shipped tools block only what the IRS would reject

The professional suites all share one design, and it explains how a known warning sat unread. **Only the e-file-reject tier hard-blocks.** In Intuit's taxonomy, "Critical" diagnostics flag audit risk but do not stop e-file, and a preparer can tick "Ignore" ([Intuit](https://accountants.intuit.com/support/en-us/help-article/diagnostics/common-questions-diagnostics-suggestions-tax/L7zsvPBVt)). In CCH Axcess, a reviewer "can sign-off on non-severe diagnostics to approve the return even if the diagnostic has not been cleared" ([Wolters Kluwer](https://www.wolterskluwer.com/en/expert-insights/onscreen-review-is-the-next-critical-stage-for-tax-workflow-improvement)). Practitioners complain openly about "silly critical diagnostics that have no impact" ([Intuit community](https://accountants.intuit.com/community/lacerte-product-discussions-2/how-override-some-of-these-silly-critical-diagnostics-that-have-no-impact-on-the-program-result-47729)). The engine's advisory warning about California AMT is therefore normal industry behaviour, not a strange defect. The products leave it to the human reviewer to notice.

The suites' generic mechanisms are real but narrow. UltraTax marks a field with a **blue chevron** when it had data last year and is blank this year, and with a **red chevron** when someone typed over a transferred value ([Thomson Reuters](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en-us/help/ultratax-cs/miscellaneous/review-tools-and-features.html)). Lacerte and ProSystem fx reviewer checkmarks **turn red when the number underneath changes** ([Intuit community](https://proconnect.intuit.com/community/lacerte-discussions/discussion/lacerte-tick-marks/00/43541)). These catch *change* and *disappearance*. None catches *never computed*. In all the documentation found, no vendor blocks sign-off on "a line the engine did not compute while its input is non-zero."

The AI review tools sit upstream of the problem. TR Ready to Review, SurePrep and Black Ore extract source data and then hand the computation to a deterministic engine ([TR](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en-us/posts/innovation/introducing-ready-to-review-thomson-reuters-agentic-ai-for-1040-prep/); [SurePrep policy](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en-us/help/sureprep/1040scan/1040scan-policy-statements/2025-1040scanverify-policy-1040-return)). Black Ore's ">99% accuracy" **[vendor claim]** has no published method and reads as extraction accuracy ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/04/29/3283985/0/en/black-ore-launches-tax-autopilot-for-broad-availability.html); [agent-finder](https://agent-finder.co/reviews/black-ore-tax-autopilot)). **No vendor publishes recall on seeded errors.** Taxmark's 13-of-27 versus 22-of-27 comparison is evidence the market itself lacks, which makes it worth presenting with its method stated.

## The IRS and the auditors catch novelty by demanding an independent expectation

The IRS runs four layers, and three of them are generic. **Math verification recomputes the return**, and "the computer stops verifying the taxpayer's information at the point of error" ([IRM 21.5.4](https://www.irs.gov/irm/part21/irm_21-005-004r)). **AUR matches every information return against the return line it should reach** ([IRM 4.19.3](https://www.irs.gov/irm/part4/irm_04-019-003r)). **DIF scores returns as statistical outliers against peers**, using formulas retrained on random NRP audits ([GAO/GGD-99-30](https://govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GAOREPORTS-GGD-99-30/html/GAOREPORTS-GGD-99-30.htm); [NTA Purple Book](https://taxpayeradvocate.irs.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/ARC24_PurpleBook_08_MiscRecs_66.pdf)). The fourth layer, MeF business rules, is pre-enumerated. The scale is real, at about **2.7 million math errors** on tax-year-2022 returns ([IRS Data Book](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-prior/p55b--2024.pdf)), but that is volume, not recall. One caveat matters here: arithmetic recomputation would **not** have caught California AMT. A line held at 0 is arithmetically consistent, so only a computation that actually models California AMT disagrees with it. The FTB's equivalent machinery was not researched and is unsourced.

Audit standards turn this into a procedure. ISA 520 requires the auditor to "develop an expectation of recorded amounts" that is precise enough, and to "determine the amount of any difference … acceptable without further investigation" before looking at results ([ISA (NZ) 520](https://standards.xrb.govt.nz/standards-navigator/isa-nz-520/)). It also says that "as the assessed risk increases, the amount of difference considered acceptable … decreases" ([FRC ISA 520](https://www.frc.org.uk/docs/2453/html)). The PCAOB's 2024 proposal goes further: the auditor must form its own expectation "and not rely on the company's amount" ([CPA Practice Advisor](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2024/06/13/pcaob-looks-to-clarify-rules-on-substantive-analytical-procedures/106774/)). For Taxmark, the company's amount is the engine's output. An expectation built from the ISO adjustment and California's AMT rate, compared with a recorded 0 against a threshold set in advance, would have breached any reasonable threshold.

Close and audit tools apply the same closed-world idea at the account level. FloQast and BlackLine **cannot certify an account that does not tie out** within a set threshold ([FloQast](https://help.floqast.com/hc/en-us/articles/360002045372); [BlackLine](https://www.blackline.com/blog/improve-automate-your-account-reconciliation-process/)). Caseware's step AI-20 requires trial-balance totals to agree with transaction detail ([Caseware](https://docs.caseware.com/2020/WebApps/31/nl/Explore/Audit/Risk-response.htm)). DataSnipper links every workpaper cell to its source, so an unreferenced cell is visibly unsupported ([DataSnipper](https://datasnipper.com/resources/busy-season-document-matching)). MindBridge, the most "AI" of the group, scores every entry with named control points and shows "which control points have triggered" **[vendor claim]** ([MindBridge whitepaper](https://info.mindbridge.ai/hubfs/docs-website/Whitepaper/MindBridgeAnalytics-AI-Audit-Methodology-Whitepaper.pdf)). Explainability in these tools means naming the test that fired, not a free-text rationale.

## Spreadsheet auditors already named this bug: a formula overwritten by a value

Spreadsheet research supplies the closest analogue and the best numbers. In a seeded-error test, structural auditing tools found up to **84% of planted errors, against 47% for Excel's built-in auditing**. Formulas overwritten with values were "easily identified". SpACE caught a broken chain because of its "failure to establish a link between the source data and the bottom line cell" ([Nixon & O'Hara](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1001.4293)). That broken chain is the California AMT case: 40,000 entered the AMT path and stopped. ICAEW's advice on unused inputs applies directly: an unreferenced input "is not necessarily an error, but it does beg the question 'why is it there?'" ([ICAEW](https://www.icaew.com/technical/technology/excel-community/excel-community-articles/2022/how-to-review-a-spreadsheet-part-4)). Hand-built calculations carry uncorrected errors in about **2–5% of formulas** ([Panko](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0801.1514.pdf)). In a return of a few hundred engine lines, unanticipated errors are therefore expected, not exceptional.

Two more designs show what "never computed" should look like as data. The IRS Direct File **Fact Graph** has only two kinds of fact, user-entered and derived. An unknown input yields a result "marked as incomplete", and anything built on a placeholder default stays incomplete too ([Fact Graph tutorial](https://github.com/IRS-Public/direct-file/blob/main/direct-file/fact-graph-scala/shared/src/main/scala/_tutorial/02_completeness.worksheet.sc)). **HabuTax** raises an error on any unimplemented calculation instead of producing a silently wrong return ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/habutax/)). Soda reports a check that could not run as `[NOT EVALUATED]` rather than as a pass ([Soda](https://docs.soda.io/soda-cl/anomaly-detection.html)). The lesson is the same in all three: **zero is a value, and "not computed" must be a different state**. A community issue on a Direct File fork shows where silent passes hide, in placeholder defaults of false or 0 ([issue #186](https://github.com/jz4396/direct-file/issues/186)).

Profiling tools such as Deequ and Monte Carlo help less. They infer constraints from observed data and "assume the data is static and correct" ([Deequ](https://github.com/awslabs/deequ/blob/master/src/main/scala/com/amazon/deequ/examples/constraint_suggestion_example.md)), and one household's return has almost no history to learn from. Their transferable idea is narrow: a line that was non-zero in every comparable version should alarm sharply at 0 ([Monte Carlo](https://docs.getmontecarlo.com/docs/metric-monitors)).

## The layer to add: four structural gates, in priority order

Taxmark computes nothing itself, and that constrains the design. It cannot nudge an input and re-run the engine. It does hold every line's value, rule id, inputs and sources, plus three computed versions and prior years. Every gate below works on that data. The "would have caught" column is assessed only against the California AMT miss. Which of the other 27 known mistakes each gate catches has not been measured; it should be measured by running each gate against the ground-truth ledger.

| # | Layer | Rule, in one sentence | Would it have caught CA AMT? | Rough cost (estimate) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Line-status gate** (generalise CHK-LINE-NOT-COMPUTED) | Every filed line has status complete, placeholder, incomplete or not_computed. Load fails for a line with neither inputs+rule nor a source. Approval is blocked unless every filed line is complete or a reviewer signs it off with a reason | Yes, via the rule text admitting non-computation. Also catches a constant 0 with non-zero direct inputs even when the rule text admits nothing | About a day on top of what shipped |
| 2 | **Input reachability and disposition** | Every non-zero input must reach at least one filed line, and every federal adjustment must carry a disposition for each state in scope (conform, adjust, not applicable) | Yes: the 40,000 ISO adjustment reaches federal AMT but has no California disposition | 1–2 days, plus a small per-jurisdiction "should reach" map, which is the only tax-specific part |
| 3 | **Version-diff influence check** | Across the three computed versions, if a line's direct input changed and the line did not move, flag it, unless the rule's gating explains why | Only if the ISO input differs between versions; otherwise silent. Cheap, and catches "declared lineage, no effect" | About a day |
| 4 | **Independent expectations** (ISA 520 style) | For a short list of high-value lines (tax, AMT, credits, state tax), compute an expectation by a route that does not use the engine's rule, with the threshold fixed before comparing, and a zero threshold for lines the engine flags as uncertain | Yes: expected AMT from 40,000 × rate versus recorded 0 | 2–3 days for about ten lines |

Gates 1 and 2 matter most because they are **closed-world**. They encode no knowledge of AMT and fire on any line or input that fails to account for itself, which is exactly what BlackLine, Caseware AI-20 and the Fact Graph do. Gate 2 carries a design detail worth keeping: dispositions work like double entry, because every input must be "posted" somewhere. Each gate also needs a seeded test proving it can fire, following the global rule that a zero must be proven able to be non-zero. A deterministic check that fails silently on unexpected data shape is a documented failure ([ideascripting forum](https://ideascripting.com/node/1205)).

A **second engine** is gate 5, and it belongs later. PolicyEngine-US (AGPL, very active) and OpenTaxSolver (2025 release, includes California) are the plausible candidates. Neither is confirmed to model the 540NR part-year return or Schedule P California AMT ([PolicyEngine](https://github.com/PolicyEngine/policyengine-us); [OTS](https://opentaxsolver.sourceforge.net/download2025.html)), and TAXSIM's state law is dated to 2018 ([NBER](https://taxsim.nber.org/)). A second engine also fails where the first one does. Knight and Leveson found that independently written versions failed together "substantially more than expected", on the same hard parts ([Knight & Leveson](https://dspace.mit.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/1331578f-65c1-4665-a0be-1bc3cdc001d5/content)), and a 2026 preprint reports the same with AI coding agents ([arXiv 2606.20158](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.20158)). Hard parts like California AMT are exactly where this matters. Its value is mostly coverage: when "engine B cannot compute this line", that is itself a deterministic flag. Estimated cost is one to two weeks of mapping work, with uncertain payoff.

Metamorphic testing has a published record on tax software, including "missing eligibility conditions" found in OpenTaxSolver ([Tizpaz-Niari et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.04998v2)). The method is to raise an input and assert that the output must not fall and must move. It needs a callable engine, which Taxmark does not own. Gate 3 is the version of it Taxmark can run today. A real metamorphic suite belongs with whoever owns the engine.

## Agentic AI earns a seat beside the checks, not above them

The evidence supports making the AI layer agentic, within limits. Every vendor and Big Four rollout uses the same split: an LLM routes, picks what to examine and drafts review notes, while engines and ledger tests compute ([OpenAI/Basis](https://openai.com/index/basis); [ACCA](https://abmagazine.accaglobal.com/global/articles/2026/aug/practice/audit-s-not-so-secret-agents.html)). Unaided models compute returns poorly. The best TaxCalcBench model got **32% of federal returns exactly right**, consistency fell across repeated runs, and the authors conclude deterministic engines remain necessary ([TaxCalcBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.16126v1)). The blind run succeeded because it *recomputed with a Python tool* and compared, which avoids those failure modes. One run is one sample, though. Before quoting a recall figure, run it several times and report the union, the intersection and the spread.

**Learning from past findings and writing its own rules** is where the real gain lies, and also the real risk. The working pattern is Meta's TestGen-LLM: the LLM proposes, deterministic filters dispose, and a human accepts. Candidates that did not build, flaked or added nothing were discarded, and engineers accepted **73%** of the survivors ([arXiv 2402.09171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09171v1)). Libraries of verified, executable skills measurably improve agents ([Voyager](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.16291); [AWM](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/wang25bx.html)). The documented failure is the **oracle problem**: LLM-written checks encode what the code *does*, not what it *should* do ([arXiv 2410.21136](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.21136v1)), and they trail human-written ones on mutation score ([Molinelli et al.](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~mernst/pubs/neurosymbolic-oracles-ase2025-abstract.html)). A rule an agent induces by watching this engine could enshrine "California AMT is 0" as correct. The promotion gate is therefore fixed. A proposed rule is promoted only if it is pure code with no model call at run time; **fires** on the packet where the error was found and **stays silent** on a corrected copy and on a synthetic variant; is stated over field types rather than this household's amounts; cites IRC or the form instructions, never the engine; and is accepted by a reviewer. It then lands as a regression test in `project-build/tests/`.

Regulation points the same way. IRS OPR Alert 2026-19 tells practitioners to "thoroughly review all AI-created material" ([Journal of Accountancy](https://www.journalofaccountancy.com/news/2026/jun/irs-outlines-ai-risks-circular-230-duties-for-tax-practitioners/)). The PCAOB requires auditors to investigate items that technology tools flag ([PCAOB](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/implementation-resources-PCAOB-standards-rules/amendments-related-to-aspects-of-designing-and-performing-audit-procedures-that-involve-technology-assisted-analysis-of-information-in-electronic-form)). Both mean an agent finding needs a review state (open, confirmed, dismissed with a reason) and the same schema as any check: source document, field, recomputation and authority.

What not to do follows from the evidence. Do not let the agent block approval directly, and do not let it auto-promote rules. Do not let it compute return lines. Do not add a battery of low-precision anomaly detectors: auditors shown higher false-positive rates became less skeptical ([Barr-Pulliam et al.](https://scholarspace.manoa.hawaii.edu/handle/10125/70450), venue unconfirmed), which is the same way a "not computed" admission sat unread. Do not treat "all checks green on the engine's own data" as assurance: full-population testing on client data made auditors *less* responsive to an external red flag ([Gold et al.](https://www.ieseg.fr/en/events/conference-an-unintended-consequence-of-full-population-testing-on-auditors-professional-skepticism/), abstract only). And do not quote any layer's recall as a percentage beyond Taxmark's own measured packet. No source supports such a figure for any product.

## Conclusion

The California AMT miss changes what the product is. The 15 check families answer "is this a mistake we have seen?", the structural layer answers "can every number account for itself?", and the agent answers "what has neither layer thought of yet?" Only the middle question generalises to the next household for free. Its gates are cheap, a few days in total by this estimate, because Taxmark already stores each line's rule, inputs and sources. Most competitors cannot ask that question, because their diagnostics block only what the IRS would reject.

The agent's long-term value is as a rule factory more than as a reviewer. Each confirmed finding that survives the fire-and-silence gate becomes a cheap, reproducible deterministic check, so the agent's job keeps narrowing to what is genuinely new. The open measurement that matters is not the agent's recall on its own. It is how many of the 27 known mistakes the structural gates catch with no AI at all. That number shows how much of the agent's 22 was doing work that structure should have done.
